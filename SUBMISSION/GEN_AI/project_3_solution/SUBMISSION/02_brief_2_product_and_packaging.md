# Brief 2 — Product & Packaging

*AURA Cold Extraction — premium ready-to-drink coffee.*

### Packaging Direction

**Material and structure**
- Body: heavy-wall aluminium sleek can, 8.5 oz, 204 profile, matte soft-touch lacquer over a continuous obsidian wrap.
- The unprinted band: the top third of the can carries no print at all — bare brushed aluminium, sealed but unfrosted. This is the only structural idea the brand insists on (see below).
- Closure: raw aluminium full-aperture lid with a wide tab. No plastic lip guard, no bolt-on mechanism: nothing that would need explaining.
- Label: uncoated, fibrous paper collar (not a gloss shrink sleeve) wrapped over the lower body, opening along a perforation so the panel can be read flat and kept.
- Facts etched, not printed: batch code, extraction date and the cold-chain window are laser-etched into the base ring so they cannot be reprinted or worn away.
- Secondary: 4-pack is a plain unbleached-board tray, no window, cold-case-ready; nothing about the carton should compete with the can.

**Colour and finish**
- Ground: obsidian (deep near-black matte). Mark and type: bone white.
- One warm accent only, used for the temperature line: a faded terracotta-ochre that echoes the kraft/clay family the category uses to signal agriculture, but applied as a single hairline rule rather than a background (Part 4 · Packaging Formats and Semiotic Significance).
- Finish discipline: matte soft-touch on print, raw metal left raw, one micro-embossed detail on the collar (the batch number), no foil, no gloss, no gradient, no holography (Part 4 · Material and Tactile Sensoria, Part 4 · Material and Tactile Sensoria).
- Why this colour: the research's colour-discipline table lists the super-premium palette explicitly — raw silver, matte bone-white, slate grey, abyssal obsidian — against the commercial browns and neons of the mass tier (Part 7 · Q2). Board reference: MB-002 (Nordic stillness) with MB-006 (condensation macro) as the photographic companion.

**Shelf standout at three feet — The unprinted frost band.**
The can is sold from the refrigerated bay, so the bare aluminium band leaves the case below dew point and frosts. From three feet, in a wall of minimal cans, it is the only object that looks colder than its neighbours and the only one that changes while you watch it. The category prints its restraint; this one behaves.

Above the collar, the frozen band carries the temperature line in a single line of type, so the 'why is it frosty' question answers itself in one second.

**Front-of-pack rule** — Three elements only, the research's minimal front-of-pack rule: brand mark, product category, and one distinguishing parameter (Part 4 · Label Information Architecture).
Mark / 'COLD BREW — 8.5 FL OZ' / the parameter line '18 h · 38°F · 148 MG CAFFEINE'.

### Functional-to-Emotional Bridge

The band is not decoration; it is the part of the can we left bare because it is the part that behaves. Taken out of the cold case it frosts, cooling the hand before the first sip and giving the drinker the only proof in this category that needs no claim: the can is cold, so the coffee was kept cold. That one decision — less print, more material — is the philosophy made physical. It carries the brand's promise (nothing added, nothing hurried) into the fingers before it reaches the tongue, and it reframes the price: the $5.50 is not buying a mood, it is buying an unbroken chain of custody that starts in the case and ends in the hand.

### Shelf / Competitive Differentiation

Placed beside the researched leaders, the package is not better dressed — it is differently governed. Each competitor below owns a visual territory; AURA's territory is behaviour over time.

*Chosen set against the researched competitors (research pack, competitive matrix Part 2 · Competitive matrix)*

| Competitor | Their signature move | What is unmistakably different about ours |
|---|---|---|
| Blue Bottle (Part 2 · Brand profile) | Raw brushed-aluminium can, cerulean silhouette, museum-grade emptiness | Our metal is unprinted and exposed only where it frosts; theirs is printed and stays visually identical in or out of the case. |
| La Colombe (Part 2 · Brand profile) | Milk-white can, patented nitrogen froth, café draft theatre | No engineered mechanism and no café mime: the tactile event happens to the pack because of where it is stored, not because of what we built into it. |
| Stumptown (Part 2 · Brand profile) | Amber glass apothecary heritage and indie credibility | We deliberately avoid the heritage register: our cold is thermodynamic, not nostalgic. |
| Rise (Part 2 · Brand profile) | Radiating sunburst, high-key optimism, oat-led energy | No sunburst, no energy promise: our warmth is a single hairline, our claim is a temperature. |
| Califia (Part 2 · Brand profile) | Pastel carafe curves and botanical softness | Straight-wall geometry and one colour: no curves, no botanicals, no lifestyle softness — the pack is a container, the drink is the subject. |
