# Brief 1 — Brand Idea & Philosophy

*AURA Cold Extraction — premium ready-to-drink coffee.*

### Core Idea

AURA is a cold-chain cold brew: one micro-lot, slow-pressed, never heat-treated, poured into a two-minute ritual that belongs to whoever opens it.

It is the brand that keeps the promise the packaging usually breaks — the temperature on the can is the truth on the can.

> Research basis: Part 7 · Q1; Part 7 · Q4; Part 7 · Q6.

### Philosophy / Point of View

The category has spent a decade arguing that it is more crafted than the can beside it. Almost none of it will tell you what temperature it was kept at, or how long it was cold before you bought it. That silence is the whole business: freshness is treated as a design cue rather than a fact.

AURA believes freshness is a temperature, not a claim, and that a ritual is the proof — a small repeated act with a time and a temperature attached. Where the category markets the result, we show the two minutes. Where it promises a mood, we print a process: extraction hours, holding temperature, exact caffeine, batch code, price paid at origin.

This answers the research gap directly: buyers want verifiable craft, not another minimal can. Our POV is deliberately unglamorous and hard to copy — a competitor can imitate our palette in an afternoon; they cannot imitate a cold chain in a weekend.

> Research basis: Part 7 · Q1; Part 7 · Q3; Part 7 · Q4; Part 7 · Q5.

### Positioning Statement

**For** urban professionals who already read the back of the can — hybrid workers aged 26–45 who have replaced a daily café run and check a panel before they buy —

**AURA** is the cold-chain cold brew

**that** keeps and prints its cold chain, so the taste in the can is the taste on the label,

**because** the cold chain is the only claim in this category a buyer can actually experience: they hold it, they feel it, they drink it within the hour.

### Name Rationale

The name is **AURA Cold Extraction**. An aura is what a thing gives off before it is handled: heat radiates and announces itself, cold withdraws and asks you closer. The name carries the brand's register — quiet authority, a substance with a temperature — and it reads correctly in the two places it will be seen most: on a matte can at three feet and as a line of type above a pour.

It is deliberately not a heritage name and not a place name: the research shows the heritage register has already been absorbed by the category (Part 7 · Q3, Part 7 · Q3), and a geographic name would invite sourcing claims we would rather make with numbers.

'Cold Extraction' does the positioning work the name alone cannot: it says process, not mood, and it sets up the one promise the pack can prove.

### Logo Direction (a brief for the mark — not the mark itself)

**What it must communicate**
- Temperature before flavour: a mark that reads as cold, precise and handled rather than cold and clinical.
- A repeated action: legible as something used, not something announced.
- Restraint: one mark, one weight, one colour, no badge, no crest, no flourish.

**Visual styles from mood board**
- Monastic ritual restraint — symmetry, generous quiet space, nothing decorative (Part 2 · Brand profile, Part 5 · Film, Motion, and Sonic Branding). — board *MB-005*
- Wabi-sabi irregularity — one deliberately imperfect detail (a slightly cut curve or an off-centre dot) that reads at close range (Part 2 · Brand profile, Part 4 · Typography). — board *MB-001*
- Nordic stillness — deep graphite ground, bone-white mark, no gradient or gloss (Part 4 · Photography and Art Direction Codes). — board *MB-002*

**Explicitly avoid**
- The category default: clinical all-lowercase geometric sans-serif marks with wide tracking, the look most competitors already use (Part 4 · Typography, Part 7 · Q3).
- Heritage sign-painter or distressed gothic type: absorbed by the category (Part 4 · Typography, Part 4 · Typography, Part 7 · Q3).
- Any literal illustration of coffee plant, cup, bean, mountain or water — the mark is a temperature object, not a farm scene.
- Badges, shields, seals, certification clusters, and any mark that cannot survive being printed 14 mm wide in one colour.

> Research basis: Part 4 · Typography; Part 7 · Q3.
