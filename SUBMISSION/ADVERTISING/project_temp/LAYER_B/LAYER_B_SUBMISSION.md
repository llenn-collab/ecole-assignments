## SLIDE 01 — THE PITCH, IN ONE BREATH

> **Every tourism campaign about this state whispers. We're the ones who turn it up.**

Karnataka has always been India's loudest state with the most to say — from the maths of Carnatic music to the mosh pits of its underground. The campaign converts that living sound heritage into a travel season.

- **State:** Karnataka, India
- **Big idea:** *Turn Up the Heritage*
- **One line:** Heritage you can hear. Scenes you can join.

**Speaker note:** open cold with the one-liner. No monuments on this slide.

---

## SLIDE 02 — WHY THIS STATE, AND WHY NOT THE OBVIOUS PITCH

Karnataka already gets sold as: tech city, Hampi's granite, Mysore's Amba Vilas mansion, coffee. All true. All printed to death — and the brief rules out the common-spot route on purpose.

What the state actually owns, and nobody sells:

1. **The classical engine room of Indian music.** Purandara Dasa — the Carnatic tradition's founding theorist — taught and rests in this state; the Mysore court collected, commissioned and preserved the music for generations; and Bengaluru today runs one of India's densest networks of concert institutions — sabhas with roots to the early 1900s and a December music season that pulls the country's listeners yearly. The great 18th–19th-century composers belong to the neighbouring Tanjavur region by birth and to this region's courts by patronage and custody — the campaign asserts custody only, and that is honest and more than enough. *(Verification: attributions and dates flagged for source-citation before release.)*
2. **A granite record of sound itself.** The Vijayanagara temples at Hampi include pillars carved with musicians, and the Vittala temple's stone columns have long been cited for the rhythms they produce when tapped — now protected from tapping, which is its own great story. *(Verification flag: claims as listed in the Tourism Department's own material; confirm before release.)*
3. **The country's live-music nerve centre.** Bengaluru has spent three decades as the place Indian rock and metal bands form, tour from and break up in — university battles of the bands, small-hall metal nights, festival history, and the surviving venue strip that outlived the pandemic. *(Verification flag: recent operating status of specific venues to be confirmed.)*

Three assets, one sentence: **in Karnataka, music isn't content. It's infrastructure you can visit.**

## SLIDE 03 — THE NICHE AND THE USP

**Niche:** sound-led experiential travel — gig weekends, instrument trails, temple acoustics, artist-led walks. Deliberately not sight-seeing, not shopping, not the common spots.

**USP statement:**
> *The only Indian state where the classical and the heavy share the same stage — a living sound heritage you can attend, not just admire.*

**Why it wins:** other destinations sell what you look at. We sell what you hear, play, and bring back as a story — and it can only happen there.

---

## SLIDE 04 — TARGET GROUP

**Primary — "Scene-seekers" (domestic, 19–29).** Metro students and young professionals in India's gig cities. Travel for lineups, not landmarks. Live inside reels, Spotify-adjacent playlists and group-chat plans. Judge inauthenticity instantly. Barrier: money and timing — need a weekend they can actually do.

**Secondary — "Roots & riffs travellers" (foreign and diaspora, 28–45).** Cultural travellers from the host country's outbound markets and from Southeast Asia/Europe who plan trips around experiences. Already know Hampi; don't know it has a scene. Barrier: they don't believe a tourism board about subcultures — so artists, not officials, do the talking.

**Tertiary — "The hosts" (Bengaluru's own gig population and musicians).** They don't need convincing; they need pride. They are the media.

**Insight that carries the campaign:** young India trusts scenes and distrusts slogans. You can't advertise a culture at these audiences — you can only hand them the mic and make sure the venue has room.

## SLIDE 05 — THREE CREATIVE ROUTES (the Week-01 elevator, resolved)

**Route A — "Turn It Up."** The state's whole heritage, re-cut as volume: from raga to rage. Pro: simple, brandable, works as a call to action. Con: could flatten into a generic loudness joke without the heritage proof.

**Route B — "Two Figures."** A signature device built on a pair: two carved stone figures, facing the same way — one classical, one electric; one holding a veena, one holding a guitar; both mid-performance, both in the same stance. The state's own iconography of paired strength, re-imagined as its two great musical traditions. Pro: owns a visual world nobody else can copy; carries the duality the research actually found. Con: needs craft to avoid museum-poster softness.

**Route C — "The Setlist State."** Itineraries presented as gig lineups; every destination an act on a bill, closing with the headline slot (the big festival weekend). Pro: brilliant planning utility, native to digital. Con: thin as an image-world for print and outdoor on their own.

**Final route (Week-02 decision):** **B's world, A's call to action, C's planning engine.** The idea leads with the paired-figures sound heritage, closes with one command — *turn up* — and converts through a setlist you can book.

## SLIDE 06 — THE BIG IDEA

> ## KARNATAKA: TURN UP THE HERITAGE
> *Heritage that gets louder the deeper you go.*

**The promise:** Karnataka's heritage isn't behind glass. It's a Carnatic lesson at 6 a.m., a stone pillar with a rhythm in it, a metal gig at 9 p.m., and a band that will tell you both belong to the same state.

**The strategy line:** we don't sell sights. We sell the state's sound — and stage the whole state as India's biggest venue.

**Why this is an integrated idea, not a slogan:** one thought, six jobs. Print holds it in detail. Outdoor builds it in sequence on the daily route. Ambient springs it inside ordinary surfaces. The microsite makes it bookable. Social hands it to the artists themselves. YouTube gives it three minutes of sound. No channel gets a resized version of another's work — each carries the idea using what only it can do. (The reference cases taught us this: a word made into a system, a low-budget idea built for its screens, a joke retold per channel — never merely re-cropped.)

## SLIDE 07 — KEY CREATIVE (Print — full-page)

**Placement:** music and culture press; weekend quality supplements; in-cinema pre-feature statics at festivals.
**Format:** full page, portrait. Fold-out variant in Week 3 (see Slide 08).

**Frame (art direction — words, not artwork):**
Full-bleed single image, composed as one continuous stone surface across the middle of the page:
- **Upper half:** macro photograph of a 14th-century temple carving — a stone musician's hand, fingers caught mid-phrase on a granite string instrument, warm light, chisel marks visible.
- **Lower half:** macro photograph of a guitarist's hand at the exact same angle, fretting the same phrase on a worn electric guitar, stage light, callus and string wear visible.
- The stone edge becomes the seam of the page. A single graphic element: a thin volume-meter strip rising vertically across the join, at 70%.
- **No brand mark invented here** — destination lockup sits at bottom-right, applied per the official identity guidelines by the client's own brand team.

**Copy:**
> **Headline:** KARNATAKA HAS BEEN HEAVY FOR 600 YEARS.
> **Deck line:** The stone pillars of Hampi still keep a rhythm. So do its crowds.
> **Body (45 words):** Purandara Dasa wrote the maths. The Mysore courts kept the score. Bengaluru built the amps. One state has run India's serious sound for six centuries — and still books it nightly, in temples and small halls alike. The heritage is live. The next act is optional. You decide.
> **Endline:** TURN UP THE HERITAGE. Plan your weekend: **turnuptheheritage.in** *(campaign microsite — name to be registered/cleared)*

**Why this is the key creative:** it contains the whole idea in one glance — the depth, the duality, the proof — and every media execution on the following slides unfolds one part of it.

## SLIDE 08 — TRADITIONAL MEDIA 1 of 3: PRINT (the long read)

**What only print can do:** hold still, reward attention, carry detail and texture. A screen skips; a page dwells.
**The job here:** the museum-grade single-image read plus a 45-word essay. Print is where the credibility lives — so print is where the history is allowed to be small-print smart, not shouty.
**Execution set:**
1. Key creative as full-page (Slide 07) in music and culture titles.
2. **Gatefold variant:** fold-out completes the page into a poster — the folded page is the classical half, opening it delivers the heavy half. The physical act is the message: *unfolding = turning up.*
3. Magazine back-cover strip version; one number per issue, one picture, no body copy — the volume-meter strip at a higher level each issue, resolving at the last.
**Integration proof:** strip the copy and it is a template banner — but that is the point of an adaptation and the death of one. The gatefold's mechanism dies in a resize; that mechanism is the campaign.

---

## SLIDE 09 — TRADITIONAL MEDIA 2 of 3: OUTDOOR (the daily route)

**What only outdoor can do:** repetition at commute scale, sequence across panels, context you can read because you are standing in it.
**The job here:** build the crescendo on the routes where the audience already is.
**Executions:**
1. **"Sound Barrier" six-panel sequential** on the airport-approach corridor in the primary cities: six hoardings, one image family (the paired hands), the volume-meter strip rising panel by panel, copy at half volume — *"India's deepest music heritage."* — to full at panel six — *"KARNATAKA: TURN IT UP."* Drive time carries the idea the way a set carries a crowd: in steps.
2. **Metro and bus shelter takeover, gig-monthend only:** shelters within walking distance of real venues wear that week's actual lineup on the poster — the city's poster wall, curated. Legal framework: venue-shelter adjacency agreements *(procurement: verify slots and policy)*.
3. **Airport arrivals light-box:** *"LAND LOUD."* — plus the weekend's one live event worth catching a cab for.
**Integration proof:** no social resize survives a six-panel drive sequence, and a hoarding can't hold a 45-word essay — which is exactly why the essay stayed in print and this media got the volume-meter and the lineup glass.

---

## SLIDE 10 — TRADITIONAL MEDIA 3 of 3: AMBIENT (the surprise in the routine)

**What only ambient can do:** ambush ordinary surfaces, convert public waiting into public remembering, and earn its own news coverage.
**Executions:**
1. **"Monitor Shelter":** one iconic bus stop in Bengaluru reclad as a giant stage monitor wedge — not for loudness, for a low 20-second loop between announcements: venue owners, sound engineers and band veterans each saying one true line about the city's sound. *(Permits and municipal liaison flagged in assumptions.)*
2. **"Reverb Steps":** the staircase outside a music-college block, each riser painted with one note of a raga phrase; walking up plays it, walking down plays it back. Safety: paint only, no fixtures; footfall-friendly surfaces.
3. **"The Longest Setlist":** a wall-length vinyl-label graphic at a park-side hoarding — one continuous list: teachers, composers, venues, eras, crowds. Ends with a space labelled *"You — this weekend."* with the URL.
**Integration proof:** each of these dies the moment it is photographed and reposted as "a campaign image" — their value is the happening, which we then feed *into* the other media as earned coverage, never as recycled key art.

---

## SLIDE 11 — DIGITAL MEDIA 1 of 3: MICROSITE — *SceneMap Karnataka*

**What only a site can do:** hold depth, answer questions, convert intent into plans.
**The job here:** turn fascination into an itinerary in under 90 seconds.
**Build (deliberately small enough to actually exist):** one responsive page —
1. **The Map:** venues, instrument makers, music districts, temple acoustics; each pin a 60-second card — two facts, one sound clip, one photo.
2. **The Lineup:** the live calendar, pulled from venue partners' feeds — no invented events; the tool lists only what is real on the day.
3. **"Choose your volume" itinerary builder:** three dials — Curious / Devoted / Feral — return a two-day plan (classical morning, trails, gig night) with booking links.
4. The key film (Slide 13) embedded; the print essay readable; one share button that pre-writes the reel caption.
**Guardrail:** no custom tech promises beyond a map, a feed and three dials. Everything demoable in the room.

---

## SLIDE 12 — DIGITAL MEDIA 2 of 3: SOCIAL (the mic goes to the scene)

**What only social can do:** let the community say it, in formats that belong to the platform, with the algorithm as the A/V crew.
**Executions:**
1. **"Raga to Rage" takeover series:** six artists trained in Carnatic each post a 15-second clip that starts in a classical phrase and ends in their own heavier idiom; the campaign account only hosts and amplifies. Six weeks, one drop per week, each cut natively per platform (Reels/Shorts/feed-video treated as three edits, not one upload).
2. **Duet challenge — #TurnUpTheHeritage:** the campaign publishes a 4-bar phrase (a classical line rendered by the state's most-followed guitar maker); anyone, anywhere adds their part; best replies get a weekend pass and a pin on the SceneMap "crowd stage."
3. **Pitcam loop:** venue-partner filmed 30-seconds-of-thursday, posted the same night, geotargeted to the city the weekend of the next gig.
4. **Paid plan:** geo + music-affinity targeting around the primary metros, flighted Wed–Fri into weekend intent. Spend split 40/40/20 organic-amplify:challenge:conversion — *(budget envelope to be agreed with client)*.
**Integration proof:** nothing here is the print ad resized; social's asset is the scene's own voice, which no other media can host.

---

## SLIDE 13 — DIGITAL MEDIA 3 of 3: YOUTUBE (the three-minute proof)

**What only long-form video can do:** carry sound design honestly, hold narrative, and be searchable years after the flight ends.
**Executions:**
1. **"SOUNDWALKS" — six films, three minutes each.** One artist, one place, one original piece, performed once: a veena at dawn by the granite chariot; a qawwali-heritage vocalist in a old city lane; a thrash band on a Western Ghats pull-out; a choir in a hill-station church; a producer at a instrument workshop where the guitars are made; a finale cut intercutting the first five back to back until the classical and the heavy share one beat. **Sound-first grade:** stereo and binaural masters; the mix is the ad.
2. **15-second bumpers** cut from the loudest transition in each film, skippable ads, mid-roll on live-music content; the last frame is the volume-meter motif rising to full.
3. **Pre-roll logic:** serve the Hampi film to heritage-curious searchers, the ghats-band film to gateway-city travellers; same campaign, different films — because the media, not the audience's patience, chooses.
**Integration proof:** a resized poster can't hold a three-minute mix; the films are the only media where the promise (the sound) is literally delivered at full volume.

## SLIDE 14 — THE INTEGRATION PROOF (one idea, six jobs)

| Media | Its unique grammar | What the idea does there | Adapt test — why this can't survive a resize |
|---|---|---|---|
| Print | Stillness, detail, ownership of the page | The paired-hands image + 45-word essay; the gatefold's physical reveal | The essay dies at thumbnail; the fold is the mechanism |
| Outdoor | Repetition, sequence, commute context | Six-panel crescendo on the drive; lineup glass at shelters | A single hoarding can't hold six; sequencing needs the road |
| Ambient | Surprise inside routine surfaces | Monitor shelter, reverb steps, longest setlist wall | Exists only in place; coverage of it is earned, not bought |
| Microsite | Utility, depth, conversion | Map, real-lineup calendar, volume dials | It's a tool; posters can't book itineraries |
| Social | Community voice, per-platform formats | Artists' takeovers, duet challenge, pitcam loops | The scene won't perform in print's one-way voice |
| YouTube | Sound, narrative, search permanence | Soundwalk films, bumpers, matched pre-roll | The mix requires speakers; the essay requires paper |

**The doctrine, in a line:** each media gets a different *job* — credibility (print), build-up (outdoor), surprise (ambient), conversion (site), proof-of-life (social), proof-of-sound (video). The brief's whole lesson is this table: an adaptation shows the same face six times; an integration shows the same mind doing six different things.

## SLIDE 15 — ROLLOUT, MEASUREMENT, RISK GUARDRAILS

**Phasing (campaign), mapped to project weeks:**
- **T-6 → T-4 wks — "Half Volume" tease:** print and site only; the rising-meter serial; no answers yet. *(Wk-01/02 outputs: state, USP/TG, three routes, final route.)*
- **T-4 → T-1 — "The Lineup Drop":** films, takeover series, outdoor sequence, ambient installations switch on. *(Wk-02/03: key creative complete; media shortlist locked; WIP design.)*
- **Live season — "Turn Up the Heritage" weekends:** SceneMap + geo invites + pitcam loops run against the real calendar. *(Wk-04: this deck + NLET submission.)*

**Measurement (no invented baselines — proxies to be set with the client):** unaided recall in the primary metro panel; SceneMap itinerary starts and saves; venue-partner booking uplift during flight; challenge entries and their geo-mix; earned coverage count for the ambient works.

**Guardrails held from strategy:**
1. **Insight before aesthetic** — visual spectacle never leads a slide; proof does.
2. **No shock mechanics.** Provocation stays warm and celebratory; negative framing of the "safety shock" school is wrong for a tourism brand.
3. **Rights before reach** — every artist, venue and recording used under written consent; music licensing cleared before any film ships. *(Legal checklist attached in production docs.)*
4. **Real calendars only** — the site lists only confirmed events; nothing promised that can't be demoed.
5. **Creative freezes at the end of Week 3; Week 4 is assembly, not redesign.** The deck is the last thing we polish — never the idea.
