# Digital Media — Three Channels

**Deliverable:** D05 · Satisfies R09, R01, R10
**Artwork status:** written direction only

As with traditional media, each execution does **one job the other five cannot do**, and the lazy adapt is named and rejected in each case.

---

## 1. MICROSITE — "hundredyearhandshake.in"

### The execution

A single-page site. No navigation, no gallery, no booking widget above the fold.

**Step 1 — You arrive.** A dark screen. One line:

> *Someone in Meghalaya started a bridge in 1998. It is still not finished.*

**Step 2 — You are given a root.** A thin line appears across the screen. Text:

> *Tie your hand to it.*

The visitor presses and holds their finger or cursor on the line for **twenty seconds** — an uncomfortably long time on the web, deliberately. A counter runs. If they release early, the line snaps back and the site says, plainly: *That is how most things fail here. Try again.*

**Step 3 — You leave something.** After holding, they type one sentence addressed to **whoever crosses this bridge after them.** Not a review, not a caption — a message to a stranger in the future.

**Step 4 — You are given someone else's.** The site immediately shows them one message left by a previous visitor, with its date. They have now received a handshake and given one.

**Step 5 — It persists.** They get a permanent link. Every year on their anniversary, they are emailed the message they wrote and one new message written by someone since. The relationship does not end at the session.

A quiet footer links to responsible-visit information and village-council-run booking.

### The job only a microsite can do

A microsite is the only medium that can **record an individual's contribution, store it, and return it to them later with someone else's attached**. It is stateful. Print cannot remember you. Outdoor cannot address you personally. The film cannot wait a year and come back.

The twenty-second hold is the campaign's core argument compressed into an interaction: this takes longer than you want it to, and that is the point.

**Rejected alternative:** a scrolling site with the film embedded, a photo gallery and a "Plan Your Trip" button. That is an adapt — it treats the web as a container for other media rather than as a medium with its own property, which is memory.

## 2. YOUTUBE — "The Skip Button"

### The execution

A **skippable pre-roll** in which the skip button is the creative device.

**Seconds 0–5** (before skip unlocks): a fixed shot of a thin root over a river. A clock in the corner reads **"Year 1."** Voice, flat and factual:

> *This bridge needs thirty years. You can skip this in five.*

**At 0:05** the skip button becomes available. Most people skip. That is expected and designed for.

**If the viewer skips:** the last frame they see, for a fraction of a second, is the root still thin, and the words *"Year 1."* They leave the idea unfinished. The campaign has still landed: they were told something takes thirty years and they gave it five seconds. The discomfort is the message.

**If the viewer stays:** the counter accelerates — Year 4, Year 11, Year 19, Year 30 — the root thickening in the same locked frame, and at thirty years a child runs across it. Total runtime 45 seconds. The line appears:

> **You waited forty-five seconds. Somebody waited thirty years. Someone started it. You continue it.**

A second asset, for those who stayed, retargets with the full 90-second key creative.

### The job only YouTube can do

YouTube pre-roll is the only medium with a **built-in, universally understood mechanism for the audience's own impatience** — the skip button. The campaign does not fight it; it makes it the subject. The channel's most hated feature becomes the proof of the argument.

This cannot be ported anywhere. On OTT there is no skip. In cinema there is no skip. On social the scroll is analogous but anonymous — the skip button is a deliberate, conscious refusal, and that is what makes it usable here.

**Rejected alternative:** running the 90-second film as pre-roll. It would be skipped at second five with no idea delivered, and the media spend would be wasted.

## 3. INSTAGRAM — "Pass It On"

### The execution

Not a grid campaign. A **chain mechanic built on the reshare**.

A user receives, from a friend, a Story containing a single image: a pair of hands holding a thin root, with an empty second pair of hands outlined beside them and a counter reading, for example, **"Hand 4 of this chain."**

To interact, they must do two things:

1. **Add their hand.** Using the camera, they photograph their own hand in the outlined position. The template composites it into the chain image — their hand now joins the previous ones.
2. **Pass it forward.** They send it to exactly **one** person, not to their followers. The mechanic is deliberately private and one-to-one.

The counter increments. The image accumulates hands — 4, 9, 23, 61 — each one real, each one added by a person who then handed it to a single named other person.

Chains that reach 30 hands (one per year of a bridge's growth) unlock a real outcome: **the village council plants a new root in the chain's name**, and every participant in that chain receives the planting date and location. Their names go on a list held by the village.

Chains break constantly. That is honest and it is kept — a broken chain shows the message *"This one stopped at hand 12. Most do."*

### The job only social can do

Instagram is the only medium where **the audience is the distribution mechanism, and where a message can be passed person-to-person with identity attached**. The handshake is not depicted — it is *executed*, between two real named people, repeatedly.

Crucially, the one-to-one constraint makes it a handshake rather than a broadcast. A public post to 800 followers is not a handshake. A DM to one person is.

**Rejected alternative:** a 1:1 crop of the film with captions, posted to the brand grid. That is the textbook adapt — same asset, different aspect ratio, audience entirely passive.

## Summary — three distinct jobs

| Channel | Mechanic | Unique capability used |
|---|---|---|
| Microsite | 20-second hold, message to a future stranger, returned annually | Statefulness — it remembers the individual and reciprocates over years |
| YouTube | The skip button as the argument | A built-in, conscious mechanism for the audience's impatience |
| Instagram | One-to-one chain, hands accumulating, 30 hands plants a real root | The audience is the distribution, with identity attached to each pass |

---

## Six media, six capabilities — no overlaps

| Medium | Capability exploited |
|---|---|
| Print | Two moments on one surface, bridged by the reader's hand |
| Outdoor | Fixed public space across real elapsed time |
| Ambient | Physical participation in the daily path; collective contribution visible |
| Microsite | Memory — it stores and returns the individual's contribution |
| YouTube | The skip button — the audience's impatience made into the message |
| Instagram | Peer-to-peer transmission with identity attached |

Each capability belongs to exactly one medium in this campaign. That is the integration test, and `06_INTEGRATION_PROOF.md` runs it formally.
