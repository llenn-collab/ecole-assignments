## 1. The one-line version

> **Meghalaya is the only place on earth where you can put your hands on something that will not be finished until after you are dead — and help finish it.**

---

## 2. USP

Every other destination sells you a finished thing to look at. Meghalaya has structures that are still being grown, by hand, across rivers, and they take between fifteen and thirty years to become usable.

**The unique claim:** *the landmark is unfinished, and you can add to it.*

---

## 3. Target group

**Urban Indian knowledge workers, 28–42, metro-based.**

Defined by a working condition rather than an income band: their professional output is designed to be replaced. Code is refactored, campaigns are archived, decks are presented once.

> **The insight:** they have never made anything that will outlive them.

Secondary: international slow-travel and craft-heritage travellers (UK, Germany, Netherlands, Japan).

Full reasoning in `02_RESEARCH_AND_INSIGHT.md`.

## 4. The Big Idea

# THE HUNDRED-YEAR HANDSHAKE

In the Khasi and Jaintia hills, a person ties a fig root across a river knowing they will never cross it. Someone they will never meet crosses it instead, eighty years later.

That is a handshake between two people separated by a century. One reaches forward. One reaches back. Neither sees the other.

**The campaign invites the audience to become the second hand in that handshake — and then to offer their own hand forward to someone not yet born.**

### Why this idea and not a prettier one

It passes the only test that matters for this brief: it is a **structure**, not a slogan. A handshake has two ends, a gap of time between them, and an action that closes it. That gives every medium a different job:

- some media can hold **one end** of it,
- some can hold **the gap**,
- some can hold **the act of reaching**,
- and some can **actually complete** it.

A slogan would only be repeatable. A structure is divisible. That is the difference between an integrated campaign and an adapt campaign, which is the stated purpose of this project.

### The line

> **Someone started it. You continue it.**

## 5. Creative strategy

**Strategic posture: participation over spectacle.**

The campaign never asks the audience to admire Meghalaya. It asks them to contribute to it. Every execution ends in a transfer — something is handed from one person to another across time.

Three rules govern all six executions:

1. **No aerial hero shots of the bridges as scenery.** The moment they become a view, the idea dies. They are always shown as work in progress, usually at hand height.
2. **Every execution must contain a gap** — a physical, temporal or interactive space that the audience closes. The gap is the idea made mechanical.
3. **Every execution names a real timeframe.** Decades, not "timeless". Specificity is what makes the claim land.

**Tone:** quiet, factual, unhurried. The subject is inherently astonishing, so the delivery must be plain. No exclamation, no wanderlust vocabulary, no drone-and-swell film grammar.

---

## 6. The Key Creative

**A 90-second film: "The Second Hand."**

A pair of hands ties a young root across a river. The image holds, then the hands change — older, then different hands entirely, then younger — while the root thickens across the same frame and the same river. Four generations of hands, one continuous crossing.

At the end, a visitor's hands are guided onto the root by a village elder. The final hands are the audience's.

Full treatment, shot list, script and production notes: `03_KEY_CREATIVE.md`.

## 7. The six media executions

Each medium does **one job the other five cannot do**. This is the integration test.

| # | Channel | Type | The job only this medium can do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Print | Traditional | Holds **two moments on one surface** — the reader's own hand closes the gap |
| 2 | Outdoor | Traditional | Occupies **physical space over real time** — the poster itself grows for a year |
| 3 | Ambient | Traditional | Puts the idea **in the body** — the audience takes the physical action |
| 4 | Microsite | Digital | **Records and returns** — a personal contribution that persists and can be revisited |
| 5 | YouTube | Digital | Uses **time and skippability as the message** |
| 6 | Instagram | Digital | Makes the audience **the medium** — the handshake is passed between users |

Full executions: `04_TRADITIONAL_MEDIA.md` and `05_DIGITAL_MEDIA.md`.
Proof that these are integrated rather than adapted: `06_INTEGRATION_PROOF.md`.

## 10. A note on what is deliberately not here

No finished artwork is included. Every visual is specified as written art direction detailed enough for a designer or director to execute without further briefing. The brief asks for a campaign deck and for creatives specified across media types, and that is what this is.

Responsible-tourism constraint: the villages in question are small and the crossings are load-limited. Any real deployment must cap visitor numbers and route revenue to village councils. A campaign that damages the thing it advertises has failed on its own terms. This is treated as a design constraint, not a disclaimer — see `09_RISK_REGISTER.md`.
