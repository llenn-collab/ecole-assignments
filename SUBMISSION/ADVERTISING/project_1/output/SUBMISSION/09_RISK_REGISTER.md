# Risk Register

**Deliverable:** D09 · Satisfies R18, R21

Risks are listed with the honest weaknesses already admitted elsewhere in the submission, so that nothing is hidden between documents.

---

## 1. Campaign risks

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Over-tourism damages the crossings.** They are living, load-limited structures in small villages. A successful campaign could destroy the thing it advertises. | High if uncapped | Severe, irreversible | Hard visitor caps per site per day; booking routed through village councils; revenue share to councils; success metric is *right visitors*, not maximum visitors. Treated as a design constraint, not a disclaimer |
| C2 | **The participation promise is hollow without genuine village consent.** If visitors cannot actually contribute, the campaign lies. | Medium | Severe — credibility collapse | Village partnership is the first item on the critical path and blocks all production. If consent is not obtained, the campaign does not run in this form |
| C3 | **Cultural appropriation.** An outside agency selling a community's intergenerational practice. | Medium | High | Villagers paid at professional rates; *durbar* consent, not just state consent; villages named and credited; no staging or alteration of sites |
| C4 | **The idea is perceived as slow or worthy** rather than desirable. | Medium | Medium | The key creative is deliberately governed by craft and finish so the campaign earns aesthetic authority before asking for participation |
| C5 | **Outdoor execution fails horticulturally** — plants die, or a city refuses a living installation. | Medium-High | Medium | Mechanical fallback specified in `04_TRADITIONAL_MEDIA.md`: a painted root extended by hand monthly. The mechanic survives without the plant |
| C6 | **Instagram chains break early** and the mechanic looks like a failure. | High — chains always break | Low | Designed for: broken chains display *"This one stopped at hand 12. Most do."* Breakage is on-message |
| C7 | **The YouTube execution is judged on view-through** and looks like it underperformed. | High | Medium | Measurement framework states explicitly that view-through is not the headline metric for that asset; agreed before launch, not after |
| C8 | **Monsoon disrupts production.** Meghalaya has extreme rainfall. | High | Medium | Schedule around the window; the film's wet, overcast art direction turns the constraint into the look |

## 3. The single biggest risk

**C1 — over-tourism.**

Every other risk here can be mitigated, absorbed or fixed in a later round. C1 cannot: if the campaign succeeds too well, the crossings are damaged and the subject of the campaign ceases to exist in the form that made it worth advertising.

This is why the campaign's measurement framework deliberately refuses volume as a success metric, and why booking is routed through village councils rather than through aggregators. A state tourism campaign that treats its most fragile asset as inexhaustible has failed on its own terms, however well it performs commercially.

## 4. What would make this submission stronger

Listed plainly, in priority order:

1. Primary interviews with the target segment to validate or kill the core insight
2. Verified visitor and seasonality data from Meghalaya Tourism
3. A conversation with at least one village council about whether the participation mechanic is welcome
4. Feasibility confirmation on the living billboard from one outdoor contractor
5. A rough production budget, which this submission does not attempt
