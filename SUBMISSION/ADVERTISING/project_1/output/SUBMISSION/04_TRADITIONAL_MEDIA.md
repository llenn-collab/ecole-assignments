# Traditional Media — Three Channels

**Deliverable:** D04 · Satisfies R08, R01, R10
**Artwork status:** written direction only

Each execution below does **one job the other five cannot do**. Where a channel could have taken a resized version of the film, that option is named and rejected explicitly.

---

## 1. PRINT — "The Gap"

**Placement:** double-page spread, weekend supplements and long-form magazines (*Mint Lounge*, *The Hindu Sunday*, in-flight).

### The execution

The spread is a single continuous photograph of a river gorge, shot straight on.

On the **left page**, a pale, finger-thin root is being tied by a pair of hands entering from the left edge. Caption, small, bottom left:

> *1998. Shillong district. Bah Kyrmen ties the first root.*

On the **right page**, the **same gorge, same angle**, where the root is now a thick, walkable crossing with a person mid-step. Caption, bottom right:

> *2024. The same root. Bah Kyrmen never crossed it.*

Between them: **the gutter of the magazine.** Twenty-six years falls exactly into the fold.

The reader must physically flatten the spread with their hand to see the crossing complete. **The reader's own hand closes the gap.**

Bottom right, small:
> Someone started it. You continue it. — Meghalaya

### The job only print can do

Print is the only medium where two moments exist **simultaneously on one physical surface**, separated by a real object — the fold — that the reader must act on with their hand. On screen, the gutter does not exist. In film, the two moments are sequential, never simultaneous. The physical act of flattening the page is the idea, performed by the reader's body.

**Rejected alternative:** a still frame from the film with the endline. That would be an adapt. It would lose the gutter, which is the entire mechanic.

## 2. OUTDOOR — "The Billboard That Grows"

**Placement:** one site each in Mumbai (Bandra), Bengaluru (Indiranagar) and Delhi (Aerocity). **Duration: twelve months** — not a four-week burst.

### The execution

A living billboard. A steel armature is planted with a real fig sapling whose aerial roots are trained horizontally across the frame by a horticulturalist on a monthly visit.

At launch, the board is almost empty: a bare frame, a small plant, and painted text occupying most of the space:

> **THIS BILLBOARD IS NOT FINISHED.**
> Neither is the bridge it is about.
> Come back in eleven months.

A small brass plate shows the date planted and a live counter of days elapsed.

Each month the roots extend measurably. The text is progressively **painted out** as the roots take over the space. By month twelve the board is mostly plant and the only remaining text is:

> Someone started it. You continue it. — Meghalaya

A QR-free brass plaque gives a short URL to the microsite.

### The job only outdoor can do

Outdoor is the only medium that **occupies fixed physical space over real elapsed time**, seen repeatedly by the same commuter. The audience's own repeat exposure across a year *is* the twenty-year story, compressed. No other channel can make a person watch the same object slowly change on their daily route.

The billboard is not a message about patience. It **is** patience, in public, at scale, where everything else in the format screams for four weeks and disappears.

**Rejected alternative:** a static hoarding of the film's final frame. That would be an adapt, and worse, it would contradict the idea by being instantaneous.

**Practical note:** requires a horticultural maintenance contract and municipal permission for a living installation. Costed as a twelve-month media buy with a monthly service call. If a live plant is refused by any city, that site runs a mechanical version where a painted root is extended by hand each month — the mechanic survives.

## 3. AMBIENT — "The Rope Bridge in the Lobby"

**Placement:** office tower lobbies and co-working atriums in the three metros — sites where the target audience physically passes every working day.

### The execution

A single rope is strung across the lobby walkway at chest height, anchored at both ends. It begins as one thin strand.

Beside it, a simple sign:

> **A bridge is one root, then two, then a hundred.**
> **Add one.**
> Take a strand. Tie it on. Then walk under it.

A dispenser holds pre-cut natural fibre strands. Anyone can tie one on as they pass.

Over weeks, the single rope thickens into a braided cable as hundreds of people add strands. It visibly becomes a structure. A small plate reads:

> *Started [date]. Strands added: [counted weekly]. This one took you three months. In Meghalaya, it takes thirty years.*

At the end of the installation the braided cable is photographed, and the photograph is sent to every person who scanned in — plus the cable itself is donated to the partner village council.

### The job only ambient can do

Ambient is the only medium that puts the idea **in the audience's hands as a physical action in their normal path**. The person does not watch someone contribute to a structure — they contribute to one, with their fingers, on the way to a meeting. Comprehension happens through the body, not through the eyes.

It also does something no screen can: it makes the audience's **collective** contribution visible. Each person adds one strand and sees hundreds of other strands. That is the handshake, performed by strangers, at speed.

**Rejected alternative:** a lobby screen loop of the film. That would be an adapt and would be ignored, because lobby screens are furniture.

---

## Summary — three distinct jobs

| Channel | Mechanic | Unique capability used |
|---|---|---|
| Print | The magazine gutter as 26 years | Two moments simultaneously present on one physical surface, bridged by the reader's hand |
| Outdoor | A billboard that grows for 12 months | Fixed public space observed repeatedly over real elapsed time |
| Ambient | A rope the audience braids | Physical participation in the audience's daily path; collective contribution made visible |

None of these three is a resize of the film. None of them can be swapped for another. Removing any one removes a capability the other five do not have.
