# Creative Routes Considered

**Deliverable:** D07 · Satisfies R14, R19, R20

The brief requires a minimum of three creative routes at Week 01. Five were developed; three were taken to full evaluation. This document records all of them, including why the winner won and — more usefully — why the strong runner-up lost.

---

## Route A — "The Hundred-Year Handshake" ★ SELECTED

**Proposition:** you can put your hands on something that will not be finished until after you are dead, and help finish it.

**Posture:** participation. The audience joins something and passes it on.

**Strengths**
- The idea is a *structure* (two ends, a gap, an act of reaching), so it divides cleanly across six media instead of merely repeating
- Converts the audience from viewer to participant, which no competing destination can offer
- Directly answers the audience insight: these are people who have never made anything that will outlive them

**Weaknesses**
- Requires genuine village partnership to be honest; without it the participation claim is hollow
- The payoff is emotional and slow, which is harder to sell in a pitch than spectacle

---

## Route B — "Built By Hands You'll Never Shake" ▲ STRONG RUNNER-UP

**Proposition:** a craft-and-mastery story about the engineering genius of the Khasi and Jaintia method — the skill, the patience, the knowledge held in families.

**Posture:** authority and craft. The audience admires expertise.

**Strengths**
- The most visually beautiful route; would produce outstanding film and print
- Strongest on prestige and awards potential
- Very safe — mastery narratives rarely fail

**Why it was not selected**

It makes the audience an **admirer**, not a participant. That is a fundamental strategic limitation: admiration is exactly what the target group already gets from every other destination, and it does not touch the insight that they have never built anything lasting. A craft route sells Meghalaya's people as impressive. The selected route sells the audience a role.

It is also structurally weaker across six media. "Look how skilfully this was made" is a *statement*, and statements resize — which is precisely the adapt trap the brief is testing for.

**Not discarded.** This route now governs the key creative's execution standard: craft and finish lead in the film, because the film's job is to earn belief. See `06_INTEGRATION_PROOF.md` §4.

---

## Route C — "The Slow Place" ✕ REJECTED

**Proposition:** Meghalaya as an antidote to speed — a slow-living, decompression destination.

**Why rejected**

Generic. Bali, Bhutan, Kerala and half of Scandinavia already make this claim, and several make it better. It also fails the brief's exclusion test by sliding toward sightseeing and rest rather than a genuine niche. Worst of all, it wastes the root bridges entirely — under this route they become scenery, which the campaign's own rules prohibit.

---

## Route D — "Rain Country" ✕ REJECTED EARLY

**Proposition:** own the rain. The wettest inhabited place on earth, sold on monsoon extremity.

**Why rejected**

Strong and ownable, but it points at climate rather than at the niche identified in research. It would produce a striking campaign about weather that happened to be set in Meghalaya. The root bridges would be a supporting detail rather than the subject — and the bridges are the only genuinely unique asset.

Retained as a possible seasonal burst within the selected route, not as a campaign idea.

---

## Route E — "The Bridge That Isn't Finished Is You" ✕ REJECTED EARLY

**Proposition:** a self-improvement metaphor — you are also a work in progress.

**Why rejected**

Turns a real place and a real practice into a motivational abstraction. It would be true of anywhere and would insult the specificity of the subject. Also risks reading as a wellness brand rather than a state tourism campaign.

## The selection argument in one paragraph

Route B would produce the more beautiful campaign. Route A produces the more *integrated* one, and integration is what this brief grades. The decisive difference is that Route A's idea has internal parts — reaching back, the gap, reaching forward — which can be distributed across media, while Route B's idea is a single assertion that can only be restated at different sizes. Choosing B would have produced a gorgeous adapt campaign, which is the exact failure mode the project is designed to teach against.
