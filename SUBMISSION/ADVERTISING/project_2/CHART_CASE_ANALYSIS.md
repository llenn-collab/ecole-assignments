## 7. Three media-development directions to explore

These are **creative proposals authored for the brief**, not objects or outcomes discovered in the chart. They remain cause-agnostic until a cause and audience are researched. They answer the brief's request for three initial concepts without inventing a product or service.

### A. The overlooked message

Use an existing medium where the researched audience ordinarily overlooks something; change its hierarchy, placement, or sequence so a neglected social issue becomes visible in that encounter. The media use—not a separate product—carries the statement.

- Symbolic prompt: Palace 3's supplied emergence motif.
- Guard: do not assume what the audience overlooks; observe or test it.
- Validation: can the audience identify the issue and intended response without an explanation from the presenter?

### B. The interruption that clarifies

Rework a familiar media format so a brief, safe interruption demonstrates the researched problem, then immediately resolves into one clear message and action. Avoid fake emergencies, misleading claims, or inaccessible interaction.

- Symbolic prompt: the centre's information-confusion warning and Palace 7's message-reception warning, used as constraints rather than a promise.
- Guard: the interruption must not become the only thing remembered.
- Validation: compare recalled meaning with the intended message; ask about discomfort and misunderstanding.

### C. One message, encountered in context

Place a single concise message in an existing channel at the researched moment when a relevant choice is made. Innovate how the channel is used rather than building an app, platform, or service.

- Symbolic prompt: Palace 4's conditional amplification/support and Palace 1's effort/resistance warning.
- Guard: the relevant choice, channel access, and audience behaviour are unknown until researched.
- Validation: verify that the placement reaches the intended audience and that the requested action is understood and feasible.

There is no chart-certified winner. Select one only after comparing research relevance, ethical acceptability, comprehension, production feasibility, and the strength of the message.
