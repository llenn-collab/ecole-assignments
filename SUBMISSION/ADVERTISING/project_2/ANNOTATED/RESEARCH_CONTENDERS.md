# Research-led campaign contenders

Tree: ANNOTATED | Status: partial—selection stage | Fallback: false

Research accessed: 14 October 2026. This is a decision dossier, not the completed final campaign. Findings, interpretation and creative hypotheses are kept separate. Proposed target audiences are strategic choices, not measured audience segments. No interviews, audience tests, permissions or campaign results are claimed.

## Design architecture

**Make an overlooked consequence or responsibility visible through an existing medium, at a moment when the audience can take one clear action.**

The innovation must communicate rather than become a new product/service. Prefer an execution that can be demonstrated without expensive infrastructure. Avoid compulsory QR journeys, shame, simulated emergencies and unnecessary complexity. A contextual placement is not automatically innovative; the way the medium conveys the message must do meaningful work.

## A. Unequal unpaid domestic labour — preferred overall platform

### Documented facts

India's official Time Use Survey 2024 reports that, among people aged 15–59, 92.9% of females and 30.4% of males participated in unpaid domestic services for household members during the reference day. Among participants in that activity in the same age band, average daily time was 305 minutes for females and 86 minutes for males. These are national figures, not Mumbai-only findings. Source: Tables 1 and 2 of the MoSPI/PIB release dated 28 March 2025. [2](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2116301)

A second publisher reproduces the 92.9%/30.4% and 305/86 figures from the same survey. This is a cross-check of reporting, not an independent survey replication. [1](https://www.bluekraft.in/time-use-survey-a-time-for-change/)

### What the facts support

A substantial national gender imbalance in participation and time spent on unpaid domestic services. They do not establish that every household is unequal, that women do no paid work, or that a particular age/urban subgroup has the same values. Domestic services and caregiving are distinct categories; do not merge their minutes or denominators. [2](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2116301)

### Proposed focus and audience

Narrow from “gender equality” to **adult household members treating routine domestic tasks as someone else's responsibility**. Initial audience proposal: men aged 20–35 living with family or partners in urban households. This is a campaign focus to validate, not a demographic finding in the data.

### Working audience insight — hypothesis, not a research finding

**“I notice the chore when it is unfinished, not the person who keeps finishing it.”**

The behaviour to invite is ownership of one recurring task end-to-end, not thanking someone or offering occasional assistance. The survey does not measure this proposed explanation or mental load directly; interviews would need to explore it without leading respondents.

### Campaign territory: “Not done by itself”

Repurpose an existing household communication surface—such as a meal menu, society newsletter insert, or weekly household calendar—to expose the work behind an apparently automatic result and point to ownership of a specific recurring task. Printed material is temporary campaign media, not a product line or household-management service.

Provisional copy: **“Dinner doesn't clear itself. Own the clean-up, every night.”** Final wording must be tested for tone, feasibility and shared decision-making rather than imposed as a universal prescription.

### Why it contends / what could defeat it

It offers a clear route from overlooked work to shared responsibility and a low-cost contextual prototype. However, a generic “thank your mother” or familiar chore-sharing poster would not meet the creative ambition. The medium must reveal the pattern, not merely carry a slogan. Do not assign another tracking/management task to the person already doing the work. Originality and existing-campaign review are still pending.

## B. Unsafe personal listening — strongest digital demonstration

### Documented facts

WHO's fact sheet dated 3 March 2026 states that over one billion young adults globally are at risk of permanent, avoidable hearing loss due to unsafe listening. This is a risk estimate, not a count of young people already diagnosed, and not India-specific prevalence. [2](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/deafness-and-hearing-loss)

A 2022 systematic review/meta-analysis covering 33 studies and 19,046 individuals aged 12–34 estimated unsafe personal-listening-device exposure at 23.81% (95% confidence interval 18.99%–29.42%). Its global risk estimate ranged from 0.67 to 1.35 billion. The underlying reviewed studies were published during 2000–2021; this is not a 2026 campus survey. [4](https://lup.lub.lu.se/search/publication/f4feb709-4ff9-475d-a1a1-78f7d06ccc19)

WHO–ITU's safe-listening standard uses an adult reference exposure of 80 dB for 40 hours per week and recommends tracking both level and duration. A device's volume percentage is not the same thing as a measured sound level. [1](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241515276)

### Proposed focus and audience

Frequent headphone users aged 18–25 encountering music or study audio on their phones. The design addresses recreational listening exposure—not deafness as a spectacle, and not all causes of hearing loss.

### Working audience insight — hypothesis

**“I can see my battery running down; I don't see my listening exposure adding up.”**

This is a creative hypothesis about salience, not a finding measured by the review.

### Campaign territory: “Keep the next song”

Use the audio-ad format against its usual demand for attention: a deliberately quiet or silent, clearly captioned execution asks the listener to lower their next track and check existing device listening-exposure controls where available. The medium demonstrates restraint instead of delivering a loud warning. The prototype must be clearly labelled as an ad, not a broken player.

No invented app, automated hearing test, fake live decibel reading, claimed volume-key detection, or assumed streaming-platform integration. Do not simulate hearing loss or tinnitus. No actual music production capability is presumed; a captioned motion prototype and storyboard can demonstrate the concept first.

### Why it contends / what could defeat it

The medium and requested action are closely related, and the execution is feasible to prototype digitally. The risk is an overly familiar silent-ad device or an execution that users mistake for failure. It needs an originality review and comprehension test. No local prevalence or behaviour-change outcome is established.

## C. The unprotected second rider — strongest immediate action

### Documented facts and verification boundary

Reporting on MoRTH's *Road Accidents in India 2024*, Hindustan Times on 12 June 2026 gives 54,122 fatalities in the non-wearing-of-helmet category, comprising 39,470 drivers and 14,652 passengers. These are police-reported categories, not proof that every death would have been prevented by a helmet. [4](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/road-accident-deaths-increased-2-5-in-2024-from-2023-morth-report-101781204793385.html)

A copy of the ministry report is available through the International Road Federation India site; its contents identify Table 3.3 and Annexure 37 as the safety-device tables. The precise figures above have **not** been independently verified against those primary table cells in this run. They remain provisional for final artwork. [2](https://indiairf.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Road-Accidents-in-India-2024.pdf)

Do not substitute older 2023 totals as 2024 values or describe non-use as the sole cause of a crash. Do not infer college-age or Mumbai-specific numbers from the national breakdown.

### Proposed focus and audience

Adult drivers and adult pillion passengers on short, familiar two-wheeler journeys, reached before departure. “Short-trip exception” is a behaviour to investigate, not something the national fatality table establishes.

### Working audience insight — hypothesis

**“A shared ride can be treated as one person's safety decision, even though two people need protection.”**

### Campaign territory: “Two seats. Two helmets.”

Rework a permitted parking-exit or parking-ticket format into a clear paired departure message. A single recorded vehicle becomes two visibly distinct people to protect. Message: **“Two seats. Two helmets. Both fastened before you leave.”** No stickers on helmets, distraction while riding, obstruction of signs, helmet distribution service, or product endorsement.

### Why it contends / what could defeat it

The response is specific and the medium can be placed before action. The conceptual territory is familiar, so a clever pairing must do more than repeat a road-safety poster. Helmet access, fitting guidance and permission for placement cannot be assumed. Primary-cell verification is a factual gate before final data-led copy.

## D. Occupational heat protection — reserve with a real site partner

### Documented facts

ILO's occupational safety portal reports that 2.41 billion workers are exposed to excessive heat each year, citing its 2024 climate-and-work report. This is a global exposure estimate, not an India heat-death figure or a local 2026 survey. [3](https://www.ilo.org/topics-and-sectors/safety-and-health-work)

Indian Express reported the same ILO estimate in April 2024 and described India's labour ministry calling for drinking water, rescheduled working hours and other worker-protection measures. It is corroborating reporting, not an independent replication of the global estimate. [5](https://indianexpress.com/article/business/economy/over-70-workers-globally-exposed-to-excessive-heat-indias-manual-labour-at-risk-ilo-9285161/)

### Proposed focus and audience

Small-site supervisors or residential-society managers controlling outdoor work schedules. Do not put the burden of solving exposure on workers who may not control breaks or hours.

### Working audience insight — hypothesis

**“A schedule can count hours worked without making hazardous exposure visible.”**

### Campaign territory: “Put heat on the schedule”

Repurpose an existing shift notice or supervisor briefing format to make protective planning visible. Its request is to review safe scheduling and protective measures using appropriate guidance—not to launch a cooling service or invent a universal safe temperature.

### Why it is a reserve

The stakes and design opportunity are strong, but meaningful execution depends on workplace access, worker consultation and authority to change conditions. Communication cannot substitute for actual protections. Without a partner, it risks becoming a compelling demonstration detached from achievable action.

## Recommendation and decision

**Preferred overall: A, unequal unpaid domestic labour.** It has directly inspected official Indian data and a strong route from invisible work to a concrete redistribution of responsibility. This is an editorial recommendation, not a numerical proof of effectiveness.

**Preferred if digital demonstrability is the priority: B, unsafe listening.** It offers the clearest direct relationship between the communication medium and requested behaviour. Its empirical scope must remain global unless local research is added.

C is a specific action-led alternative with a primary-data verification gate. D should advance only with realistic access to an organisation controlling work conditions.

After cause selection, develop **three genuinely different media concepts within that cause**, compare them transparently, and resolve them into **one final campaign**. These four cause contenders do not replace that required ideation stage.

## Research and execution gaps

- No primary audience research or message test has been conducted; all audience insights above are labelled hypotheses.
- No causal evidence shows that the proposed executions will change behaviour.
- Two publications using the same dataset are not two independent studies.
- Selected-subgroup, Mumbai and campus prevalence remain unknown.
- Exact primary verification is outstanding for the road-safety breakdown.
- Originality/precedent review, production access, permissions and real costs remain to be established.
- A polished mockup must not be described as a deployed campaign. Research findings must not be described as the student's own fieldwork.
