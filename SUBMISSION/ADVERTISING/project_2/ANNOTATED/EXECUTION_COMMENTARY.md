# Execution commentary

Tree: ANNOTATED | Status: design complete; fieldwork not performed | Fallback: false

## Why this issue was selected

The selected issue is adult pillion helmet use. It narrows a broad road-safety theme to a specific person, omission and moment of action. The choice reflects the user's preference for this direction and the practical opportunity for a contextual communication idea. It does not assert that this is the only worthwhile social cause.

## What changed during development

A parking-slip route was considered and rejected after research found an existing receipt-format helmet campaign. The final route moves to the stationary passenger seat, with a message addressing the person about to occupy it. Precedent screening is documented but not represented as exhaustive originality clearance.

## What is finished

The submission includes a written campaign strategy and research narrative; three distinct media concepts with selection reasons; an editable presentation and PDF; A6 hero, A3 supporting poster and vertical social artwork in PDF/SVG/PNG; an illustrated contextual demonstration; a captioned 15-second HTML motion prototype; printing guidance; and blank comprehension/pilot instruments.

## What is deliberately not invented

The proposed audience insight is labelled a hypothesis. No interview quotations, local prevalence findings, test outcomes, venue permissions, deployment photographs, vendor quotations or effectiveness lifts have been fabricated. The national statistics are attributed through their actual publication chain. The short-form creative avoids compressing qualified research into a misleading headline.

## Principal creative risk

“This seat has a head” is a metaphor. The literal passenger-helmet instruction sits beneath it, but a real comprehension test must establish whether the metaphor helps or confuses. If it confuses, replace the metaphor with the direct action before investing in a larger rollout. Visual polish is not evidence of understanding.

## Principal practical risk

The campaign card must never remain on a vehicle when someone mounts or rides. Opt-in placement, an engine-off stationary setting and supervised collection are non-negotiable. If they cannot be met, use the illustrated prototype only. No installation is worth creating a distraction or hazard.

## Evaluation boundary

The provided instruments are empty because fieldwork has not occurred. The comprehension threshold is a proposed revision rule, not an industry benchmark. An optional small pilot can describe aggregate observations, but cannot establish lives saved, long-term effects or causality. Production is complete as a design proposal; permissions, field validation and deployment are separate real-world actions.
