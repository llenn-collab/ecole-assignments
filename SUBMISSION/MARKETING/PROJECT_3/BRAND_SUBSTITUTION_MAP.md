# Brand substitution map

The analysis you asked for was run with the assignment brief as its case context. That brief does **not** contain your brand or your persona — it is Assignment 2, and the brand lives in Assignment 1.

The charter the analysis runs under forbids resolving a declared unknown, so rather than inventing a brand the deliverable was written with **slots**. Every slot below is a place where Assignment-1 information goes in, and nothing else in the strategy has to change.

**One 20-minute pass fills this table and the deck is yours.**

| Slot | Where it appears | What to put in | Example shape *(from the brief's own worked example)* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BRAND** | everything | the brand name and one-line positioning from Assignment 1 | *"a better-for-you snack brand"* |
| **CATEGORY** | Slides 4, 6, 9 | the product category and the category's most common confusion | *"snack labels and what they actually mean"* |

## If your Assignment-1 brand contradicts the chart reading

That is worth saying in the deck rather than hiding, and it is the strongest possible answer to the "strategic relevance" criterion.

| If your brand is… | The tension | How to hold it |
|---|---|---|
| **loud / hype-driven** (the Gymshark register) | the chart's duty gate is a *closing* gate and its friction palaces mark alarm and argument | keep the boldness in the **display** pillar (景门, palace 8 allows showing off) and keep the *claims* plain — the axis is "loud visuals, quiet claims", not "quiet everything" |
| **community-led** | the alliance palace is real (六合) but its broadcast channel is marked broken | keep community, just make it **two-way** — questions and features, not campaign announcements |
| **conversion-first / performance-marketing-led** | the generation palace favours the quiet party and its stems sit at low stages | keep conversion, but measure it in **private** signals (saves, returns, DMs) rather than in traffic |
| **a challenger attacking an incumbent** | the centre's marker is *favourable to the guest and to attack* | this is the one reading the chart **supports** — a challenger posture is available; what the chart refuses is reading the centre as *neutral* (see `C007`, red-team `A004`) |

| A named brand | not supplied — inventing one would be a fabricated fact |
| Persona demographics | not supplied (Assignment 1) |

| Paid media, pricing, competitor analysis | no chart evidence class exists for them; they are listed as missing topics in `QMDJ_ANALYST_RUN/package/07_pattern_state_registry.json` |
| Predictions of performance | the charter forbids factual prediction; every claim is tradition-conditional |
