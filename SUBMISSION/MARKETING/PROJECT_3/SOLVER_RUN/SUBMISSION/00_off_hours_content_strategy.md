## P2-01. Content Marketing Objective

### What content needs to achieve

> Make Off Hours the brand people turn to when the evening will not switch off, and make the 10-night trial feel like the obvious next step.

### The two primary objectives

1. Build awareness, of the ritual and not of a product claim
Content puts the evening problem in plain words at the moment the audience feels the problem, and shows a simple, non-medical landing. Awareness for Off Hours means recognition of a moment, not recall of a logo.

2. Educate consumers, and own the ingredient question
Meera checks ingredients and reviews before she tries any ingested product. The brand answering those questions honestly, in its own voice, passes the trust gate and earns the ₹999 trial.

### Why these two, and why not more

| Candidate objective | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Build awareness | Primary | The category is young, and the audience does not yet believe the problem belongs to them. Awareness of the moment comes before everything else. |
| Educate consumers | Primary | The trust gate for an ingested product is the ingredient question. Education also earns a non-medical brand the authority a purchase requires. |
| Generate engagement | Secondary | Engagement is a means, never a goal. Value arrives when the conversation is real: questions and check-ins, not comment volume. |
| Build community | Secondary | Community sits in Phase 2 of our own plan and works only as a two-way channel. A headline community objective now would pull the calendar toward broadcast behaviour. |
| Generate website traffic | Secondary | Traffic matters, but the primary conversion is the trial on our own storefront, fed by discovery and depth. Traffic for its own sake is not the target. |
| Support conversion | Secondary | Conversion is the next step after this content does its job. We measure the next step. We do not lead with the next step. |

Why this matters for this brand and this audience. Off Hours does not sell sleep. Off Hours competes with the habit of not switching off. A habit changes through recognition and repetition, never through claims. Meera needs to recognise her own 10 pm in the content before she trusts a sachet at 11 pm. Awareness gives her the recognition. Education gives her permission to try.

## P2-02. Brand Story and Communication

### The core brand message, one idea

> # A landing, not a pill.

What begins when work ends is not another task to master. Off Hours is the small, simple landing at the end of a day which would not stop. Every piece of content strengthens this one idea. Every piece of content competes with the habit of not switching off, not with pharmacies.

### Tone of voice, four characteristics

| Characteristic | Meaning in practice |
|---|---|
| Calm | Never urgent, never a countdown. The evening is not a sale to close. |
| Plain-spoken | Ingredient answers in ordinary words. No clinical jargon, no wellness poetry. |
| Evidence-led | Show the label, the quantity, the reason. We state what the product does and never what the reader will feel. |
| Quietly warm | Written to one tired person, not to a market. We credit the reader with intelligence and fatigue. |

### Communication principles, always and never

Always: simple · useful · specific · honest about limits · one idea per piece · credit for the reader's own experience
Never: preachy · alarmist · medical-sounding · judgmental about how someone spends an evening · urgent for the sake of urgency · competitive sneering

On the energy of our own category. Sleep content is often either clinical or mystical, and product content is often loud. Off Hours takes a third register: the calm friend who reads labels. The loud register is not a growth asset for an ingested product. Loud is the exact quality which made Meera sceptical of gummies after one bad experience.

### Sample copy, five lines which show the register

1. "You don't have a sleep problem. You have a landing problem."
2. "Magnesium, L-theanine, chamomile. The whole list sits on the pack."
3. "No countdown. No streak. Miss a night if you need to."
4. "The 10 pm scroll is not a character flaw. The evening is unfinished."
5. "We are not asking you to sleep earlier. We are asking you to land better."

## P2-03. Content Pillars

Five pillars. Each pillar carries a function. None is a bucket named "product" or "fun".

| # | Content Pillar | Purpose | Example | Why Meera cares |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plain Labels | Education and trust | "What magnesium glycinate does, in 40 seconds, with no jargon" | She reads labels anyway. The brand does her homework with her, not at her. |
| 2 | The Landing | Awareness and recognition | "10 pm, laptop closed, brain still on", the moment, named | The description matches her night. Recognition is the first kind of trust. |
| 3 | Quiet Proof | Credibility | Unedited testimonials, including the lukewarm ones. The 10-night check-ins. | She rewards evidence and distrusts promises. Imperfection reads as honest. |
| 4 | After Hours | Depth and loyalty | The sleep-journal letter: wind-down practices for people whose work ends late | She consumes deep content on her own time. She wants the long version. |
| 5 | We Won't | Differentiation | "Six things we refuse to say about our own product" | A brand which visibly refuses things is easier to believe. Refusals stick. |

### Why these are not generic buckets

- Plain Labels exists because the ingredient question is the trust gate for an ingested product. This pillar makes the trial possible.
- The Landing is not lifestyle content. This pillar puts a name on the moment, and a behaviour-change brand enters the conversation through the moment.
- Quiet Proof is deliberately unpolished. Polished testimonials read like advertising to a review-reading sceptic. The credible version is a customer's own sentence, unedited, with edits marked.
- After Hours is the depth pillar, and the reason the audience subscribes rather than follows. Nothing in this pillar is transactional, which is exactly why the pillar carries the relationship.
- We Won't is the distinctiveness pillar. Our competitor is a habit, so our content competes on standards, never on claims.

Rotation rule. Every published piece belongs to exactly one pillar. We run pillars 1 and 3 for trust, 2 for reach, 4 for belonging, 5 for differentiation. A piece which fits no pillar does not go out.

## P2-04. Platform Strategy

Three primary surfaces, chosen on audience behaviour and justified against Meera rather than against where brands are expected to sit.

| Platform | Audience behaviour | Role for the brand | Content formats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | Discovery and unwinding. Meera scrolls Reels while her brain is still at the office. | Awareness: name the moment, show the landing | Reels (15 to 30s), carousels (label explainers), Stories (polls, question boxes) |
| YouTube | Deeper consumption. She watches explainers and deep-dives on purpose. | Education: become the brand answering the ingredient question properly | Long-form (6 to 10 min), Shorts cut from the same shoot |
| WhatsApp channel and sleep-journal e-mail (one private surface, two doors) | Consented, private, read-later attention | Quiet proof, retention and belonging. The 10-day judgement window needs a guide. | Plain-text notes, check-ins, short letters, no campaigns |

### Why these three for this brand

- Instagram hosts the moment. She is awake at 10 pm, on the surface where short ritual content fits the format. This surface is the awareness engine.
- YouTube resolves the scepticism. Ingredient explainers need minutes, not seconds, and the audience already treats YouTube as the place to decide.
- The private surface turns the trial into a habit. The 10-day judgement window is exactly the period where a guided, unhurried channel does work a public post does not do.
- The three surfaces behave as one loop. Discovery names the moment, depth answers the question, the private surface holds the first ten nights, and the referral link sends a friend back to discovery.

### Deliberately not in the plan

- Television and print. Wrong age, wrong moment, unmeasurable against our conversion objectives.
- Broad awareness bursts and mass display. Spend ahead of demand lands before trial intent exists.
- Celebrity and macro-creator blitzes. Borrowed attention and the wrong trust mechanic for an ingested product.
- Heavy marketplace-ad dependence. A listing helps logistics. A listing is not where we rent our first impression.
- Alarm-first and outrage formats. These genres run on fear and argument. For a brand asking to be consumed at bedtime, the genre is the wrong register, not merely off-brand.

## P2-05. One Idea, Different Platforms

The core idea: Night 1 of 10, the first landing.

> One small ritual, shown at the exact hour the audience feels the problem, and guided through the ten nights which decide whether the ritual sticks.

| Platform | Execution | Why the execution changes |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 25-second Reel. A kitchen at night: the laptop closed, the light down, one sachet and a glass. No voiceover, no music swell. One line on screen at the end: "Night 1 of 10." | The behaviour is passive scrolling at 10 pm. The idea needs to land visually in three seconds and stay saveable for tonight. |
| YouTube | 6 to 8 minute long-form, plus a 45-second Short. The brand walks through the sachet, what each ingredient does and does not do, and how to build the ten-night habit, including what to do after a missed night. | The behaviour is deliberate learning. Here the audience wants the full answer to the ingredient question, and depth is the payoff, not the cost. |
| WhatsApp channel and sleep-journal e-mail | One plain-text note per night, ten nights. No images, no buttons. Night 1 asks one question: "What time did your evening end today?" | The behaviour is private, opt-in attention during the judgement window. A habit grows here, not a message broadcast. |

The principle. The idea never changes: one landing, shown plainly, held for ten nights. Only the behaviour changes. On Instagram the audience sees the landing. On YouTube the audience understands the landing. In the private channel the audience lives the landing for ten days. Nothing is a dead end. Every piece points to the next surface, and the referral link returns a friend to the beginning of the loop.

## P2-07. Hero Content / Campaign Idea

Route: insight, big idea, key message, execution.

### The insight
The problem for Meera is not sleep. The problem is the twenty minutes between closing her laptop and reaching bed. She does not want a lecture about sleeping earlier, and she does not want a pill. She wants the evening to end cleanly. The scene in her head is not a bedroom. The scene is a lit kitchen counter, a laptop with the lid down, and the sense of a day which finally stopped.

### The big idea
> # The 10-Night Landing
> A ten-night ritual campaign which turns the fortnight after the trial into the content itself.

Instead of a launch week which shouts, the campaign runs inside the brand's own 10-day judgement window. Ten nights, ten small pieces, one per night, each a landing rather than a lesson. The audience is not invited to watch a campaign. The audience is invited to have ten evenings which end properly. The product arrives as a consequence of the ritual, never the reverse.

### The key message
> "We are not asking you to sleep earlier. We are asking you to land better."

### Execution, a four-part arc

| Beat | What happens | Where the piece lives |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Name the moment | "10 pm, laptop closed, brain still on", the evening problem in the audience's own words | Instagram Reels, search answer pages |
| 2. Answer the question | The label read out loud, what each ingredient does and does not do, and why this is not a sleep aid | YouTube long-form, carousel explainers |
| 3. Hold the ten nights | One plain-text note per night through the judgement window, a check-in question and no promotion | WhatsApp channel, sleep-journal e-mail |
| 4. Let the proof speak | Customers' unedited notes, including the lukewarm ones, and the referral loop back into discovery | Instagram carousel, private channel, pack insert |

Campaign calendar anchor. The ten nights are the campaign clock. Everything else in the two-week plan (P2-10) either feeds a night, documents a night, or restarts the loop for the next ten.

Why this is the right hero idea for Off Hours. The campaign converts the brand's hardest metric, the trial-to-subscription window, into content. The campaign never asks the audience to feel excited, only to try. The brand keeps claim discipline and stays visible for ten straight nights.

## P2-08. Community and Engagement Strategy

How followers become participants: we ask for something they already give somewhere else, an honest description of their own evening.

| # | Mechanism | What the mechanism is | Why someone participates |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ask-the-coach, answered in public | A recurring question box where no question is too basic, answered on camera, including awkward questions about ingredients and side effects | Joining costs nothing, no leaderboard exists to lose, and the answer is visibly for them. A sceptic's question answered straight is the strongest public proof the brand produces. |
| 2 | The Shelf | A rotating, unranked set of customers' night-routine notes and photos. Inclusion, never competition. | Curation into a set carries no risk of losing. This mechanism is the opposite of a challenge. |
| 3 | Quiet Testimony | Reviews and 10-night check-ins kept in the customer's own words, unedited, with edits marked | Their words stay theirs. For an audience reading reviews before a trial, a request for a real sentence signals respect. |
| 4 | The 10-Night Check-in | A private, opt-in nightly note during the judgement window which asks one question and expects no answer | The participant gains a reason to stay in the loop on the worst nights, exactly when a public post feels like noise. |

### What we deliberately do not run

- Competitive challenges, duets and callouts. Participation built on beating someone else is the wrong register for a bedtime ritual, and the format attracts pile-on behaviour. Pile-on behaviour damages an ingested brand fastest.
- Alarm-driven live formats. "Your sleep is destroying you" content converts attention into anxiety, and anxiety is the opposite of what the brand sells.
- Announcement broadcasts through community channels. The community channel is for conversation. A launch message pushed through the channel consumes the goodwill the message was meant to borrow.

## P2-09. Influencer / Creator Strategy

### Who the creator should be

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Type of creator | Nano and micro (roughly 5k to 75k). Practitioners and educators, not personalities. Not macro, not celebrity. |
| Content category | Practical sleep and recovery education: applied sleep psychology, wind-down practice, breath-work, the physiology of switching off. No ASMR, no sleep-hack channels, no trend-jacking. |
| Audience profile | Over-wired professionals 25 to 40 in Indian metros, the same ingredient-sceptic profile as Meera: people who read before they try and who answer questions in the replies. |
| Why they are credible for the brand | Their authority is method-led, not product-led. They explain mechanisms and habits. Off Hours borrows this authority without borrowing a claim, and this trust mechanic works for an ingested product. |
| Role in the strategy | Translation and proof, not reach. Each partner takes one pillar into their own voice. The educator carries Plain Labels. The practitioner carries The Landing. No partner reads a script about the product. Each partner explains the evening, and the ritual is present. |

### The fit test, four filters applied before any conversation

| Filter | What we check | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Teaching ratio. Does most content explain rather than react or follow trends? | We buy a habit of explaining, not a posting frequency. |
| 2 | Private-engagement ratio. Do saves, questions and DM replies run out of proportion to likes? | The real signal from this audience is private, not public. |
| 3 | Reply tone. Do comment threads read as questions or as arguments? | Argument threads are the register which damages ingested brands. |
| 4 | Disclosure comfort. Will the creator say plainly, on camera, when a piece is paid? | An undisclosed partnership loses the ingredient-sceptic fastest. |

### Two recommended creators

Creator A: Shabbir Ahmed, Sleep Psychologist (sleeppsychologist.in, YouTube: Sleep Psychologist)

Author of The Sleep Foundations. MA in Psychology. Doctoral research in sleep psychology. Based in Navi Mumbai.

His public position is "understand the mechanism, not the potion", which is the claim discipline of Off Hours spoken by someone with clinical standing. He explains why a magnesium and L-theanine evening drink is not a treatment and not a sedative, which is the exact boundary the brand must hold. His audience is the ingredient-sceptic who distrusts product claims. A brand handing him the explanation instead of a script earns credibility with this audience by association.
- Role: flagship educator partner for the ten-night series. One long-form explainer, one Q&A, and a plain-language review of the label.

Creator B: Shruti Maheshwari, co-founder, Sleep Gurukul (Mumbai)
- What she is: a Mumbai-based sleep and wellbeing coach and breath-work teacher who co-founded Sleep Gurukul, an India-focused sleep and recovery practice. The practice earned recognition at the BW Wellbeing Festival in Mumbai for excellence in holistic wellbeing and sleep, and works with individuals and corporate wellness programmes.
- Audience: Indian professionals, with a strong female, metro, 25 to 40 skew, interested in sleep, breath-work and sustainable routines rather than gadgets.
- Why this person and why this brand: She carries The Landing, the ritual, breath-work and evening-practice side of switching off, which is the emotional half of what Off Hours sells and the half a product alone will never show. Her practice is India-native, not translated from Western sleep content, which matches a brand built for late-working Indian cities. Her corporate-wellness and clinic work bridges directly into the Phase 2 partnership plan, with a partner who already holds the buyer's trust.
- Role: ritual and community partner. The guided wind-down series, the breath-work pre-roll for the ten nights, and the introduction path into Phase 2 wellness programmes.

### Before we contract, three checks

1. Category conflicts. Confirm neither partner currently works with a competing sleep or supplement brand. The point is material and remains unverified.
2. Disclosure. Agree the on-camera paid-partnership line in writing before the first shoot.
3. Follower data. We deliberately avoid selecting on follower counts. Verify current audience location, the Mumbai and Bengaluru share, before activation, because Phase 1 is a two-city launch.

## P2-10. Two-Week Content Calendar

### Cadence rationale, why this frequency and not a full grid

The plan publishes ten pieces across fourteen days, with four deliberate quiet days.

- The brand's job is a habit, not a feed. Ten nights form the campaign clock, and each night carries one idea. More posts would dilute the sequence.
- The judgement window is the asset. Days 1 to 10 are make-or-break for a trialist, so content during the window supports the ritual, not the campaign.
- Two quiet days a week are a decision, not a gap. A bedtime brand posting anxiously at midnight contradicts itself. Silence on those days is part of the message.
- Every week holds one anchor and one visual. The anchor carries depth on YouTube. The visual carries reach on Reels. Everything else points at one of the two.
- Frequency follows proof. Paid and partner content amplifies only messages the plan has already proven organically. Nothing is scheduled before earning a place.

| Day | Platform | Content | Format | Pillar | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1, Mon | YouTube | Anchor: "We read our own label out loud" | Long-form 7 min | Plain Labels | Education and trust |
| Tue | None | Quiet day | None | None | None |
| Wed | Instagram | "What magnesium glycinate does, in 40 seconds" | Carousel (5) | Plain Labels | Saves and education |
| Thu | Instagram | "Night 1 of 10", the landing film | Reel 25s | The Landing | Awareness |
| Fri | Instagram | "Ask us about the label", answers to the week's questions | Stories Q&A | Plain Labels | Engagement |
| Sat | WhatsApp and e-mail | Night 5 check-in: "What time did your evening end today?" | Plain-text note | After Hours | Retention |
| Sun | None | Quiet day | None | None | None |
| Week 2, Mon | YouTube | Anchor: "Why this is not a sleeping pill", the mechanism explained plainly | Long-form 6 min | Plain Labels | Trust and consideration |
| Tue | Instagram | "Six things we refuse to say about our own product" | Carousel (6) | We Won't | Differentiation and shares |
| Wed | None | Quiet day | None | None | None |
| Thu | Instagram | "The shelf", customers' own night notes, unranked | Carousel (6) | Quiet Proof | Trust and shares |
| Fri | Instagram | Short cut of the Monday anchor | Short 45s | Plain Labels | Reach |
| Sat | WhatsApp and e-mail | Night 10: "One thing which changed. One thing which did not." | Plain-text note and referral link | Quiet Proof | Advocacy and referral |
| Sun | None | Quiet day | None | None | None |

Anchor logic. Monday anchors open the week's question. The rest of the week answers, illustrates and holds the question. The two Saturday notes land inside the ten-night sequence, so the private channel carries the ritual while the public channel carries the story.

What the plan refuses to do. The plan does not post daily to look active, does not fill quiet days for the sake of the grid, and does not schedule a launch announcement into the community channel. When a week cannot support the anchor, the anchor moves. The calendar does not fill with noise.

## P2-11. Measurement and Success

### The metrics, each tied to an objective

| # | Objective | KPI | Healthy signal | Read on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Awareness | Reach, split into Reels reach and search-arrival reach | Rising, with search arrivals growing faster than Reels reach | Monthly |
| 2 | Education | Saves and returns on label explainers | Explainers lead the month for saves | Monthly |
| 3 | Education to consideration | Video completion rate on long-form ingredient content | Above the brand's own baseline, with the "why this is not a pill" film completing best | Monthly |
| 4 | Trust | Private interactions: saves, DM questions, channel replies | Private response at least equal to public response | Monthly |
| 5 | Conversion support | Trial starts from content, attributed from discovery and search paths, at the ₹999 entry | Growing steadily, with source mix as the measure which matters, not volume | Monthly |
| 6 | Retention | Night-5 and night-10 check-in participation in the private channel | A meaningful share of trialists reply or stay in the sequence | Every 10 nights |
| 7 | Health and stop signal | Argument threads and complaint mentions per cycle | Flat or falling. This metric is the negative indicator. | Monthly |

Seven metrics, each mapped to an objective, and one of them negative. A framework unable to fail is not a framework.

### After one month, how we would know whether the strategy works

> The strategy works when saves and returns rise, private responses match or beat public ones, the ten-night check-ins receive answers, trial starts arrive through discovery and search, and argument threads stay flat.
>
> The strategy is not working when reach rises while saves stay flat, the check-ins go unanswered, and comment threads turn into arguments. Such a pattern means the brand has drifted into the loud register. The fix is not more volume. The fix is a smaller claim, stated plainer.

### What would make us change the strategy

- Private interactions never exceed public ones across a full cycle. Then the quiet-channel investment is not paying for this audience.
- A low-key partnership note outperforms a broadcast push in the same channel. Then the broadcast instinct is wrong for this brand.
- Competitive or challenge formats produce the best trust results. Then our refusal list is too conservative.
- The depth films underperform the short films on trial starts, not on views. Then the education weighting changes.

### Closing line

Strategy first. Design second. Every creative decision here has a reason, and the reason is always the same. This brand exists to end the day, not to win the feed.
