# Creator dossier — Off Hours

**Purpose:** the two creator recommendations in the deck (P2-09), expanded to a working briefing sheet.
**Selection basis:** the four-filter fit test, applied *before* any reach consideration. Follower counts are deliberately not the selection criterion and are not used as an argument anywhere in this document.

---

## Filter results

| Filter | What passing looks like | Why it matters for Off Hours |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Teaching ratio** | Most content explains a mechanism; little of it reacts to trends | We are borrowing a habit of explaining, not a posting frequency |
| **2. Private-engagement ratio** | Saves, questions and DM replies out of proportion to likes | Our audience's real signal is private; a creator who earns saves earns the right attention |
| **3. Reply tone** | Comment threads read as questions, not arguments | Argument threads are the register that damages an ingested product fastest |
| **4. Disclosure comfort** | Will state on camera that a piece is a paid partnership | The ingredient-sceptic punishes hidden sponsorship harder than it punishes price |

## Creator A — Shabbir Ahmed, Sleep Psychologist

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Platforms** | YouTube (*Sleep Psychologist*), practice site `sleeppsychologist.in` |
| **Base** | Navi Mumbai, India |
| **Category** | Applied sleep psychology — Cognitive Behavioural Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I), ACT-I, mindfulness-based practice |
| **Credentials** | MA Psychology (IGNOU); PhD scholar researching sleep psychology; author of *The Sleep Foundations* (8 evidence-based practices grounded in sleep science and CBT-I) |
| **Public position** | Explicitly method-led: "science, compassion, and CBT-I — not sleep hacks"; his published work names the problem of conflicting advice, quick fixes and unfounded supplement claims |
| **Audience** | Adults with persistent sleep difficulty, burnout and stress; professionals seeking behavioural rather than chemical answers |
| **Filter 1 — teaching** | **Pass.** His content is explainer-led by construction; his own framing rejects hacks |
| **Filter 2 — private engagement** | **Pass (by construction, not by count).** A practice-based educator builds a client and question pipeline, not a like pipeline. *Verify current save/DM volume before contracting.* |
| **Filter 3 — reply tone** | **Pass.** Practitioner audience; questions rather than pile-ons |
| **Filter 4 — disclosure** | **Verify at contracting.** Not publicly established either way |

**Why this person + why this brand.** Off Hours is not a sleep treatment and must never claim to be. Shabbir Ahmed is the one kind of partner who can hold that line *and* make the brand more credible by holding it: he can explain what a magnesium, L-theanine and chamomile evening drink does and does not do, in clinical-sounding plain language, with the authority to say "this is a ritual, not a therapy." That is the exact boundary the brand needs a third party to draw, because a brand cannot credibly disclaim its own category. His audience is the ingredient-sceptic who distrusts claims — handed the explanation rather than a script, he reaches them as proof rather than as advertising.

**Role in the strategy.**
1. One long-form (6–8 min): what actually happens in the body during a wind-down, and where a night drink fits — and where it does not.
2. One label review: reads the Off Hours pack on camera, ingredient by ingredient, including what he would tell a client to expect.
3. One Q&A from his own audience's questions, answered without a product script.
4. Carries **Plain Labels**. Never used for a launch announcement.

**Guard.** No clinical or treatment claims, no efficacy promises, and no implication that the product substitutes for CBT-I or medical care. If a brief cannot be written without such a claim, the partnership does not proceed.

## Creator B — Shruti Maheshwari, co-founder, Sleep Gurukul (Mumbai)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Platforms** | Sleep Gurukul (LinkedIn and owned practice channels); founder-led presence |
| **Base** | Mumbai, Maharashtra, India |
| **Category** | Holistic sleep and recovery — sleep coaching, breath-work, yoga, mental wellness; individual and corporate programmes |
| **Credentials / recognitions** | Co-founder of Sleep Gurukul; listed Harvard Medical School education on her professional profile; practice recognised at the BW Wellbeing Festival in Mumbai for Excellence in Holistic Wellbeing & Sleep; co-author of the practice's book *Sleep — Your Forgotten Superpower* |
| **Audience** | Indian professionals, strongly female, metro-skewed 25–40; interested in breath-work, routine and sustainable wellbeing rather than gadgets |
| **Filter 1 — teaching** | **Pass.** Coaching and practice-led content: routines, breathing, evening structure |
| **Filter 2 — private engagement** | **Pass (by construction).** Coaching practices are built on 1:1 trust; the public following is a by-product, not the product |
| **Filter 3 — reply tone** | **Pass.** Community and wellbeing discourse; questions, not contests |
| **Filter 4 — disclosure** | **Verify at contracting** |

**Why this person + why this brand.** Off Hours sells the *ending* of a day; Sleep Gurukul teaches it. Breath-work and a guided wind-down are the most direct possible demonstration of "a landing, not a pill" — and a product cannot demonstrate a practice. Her practice is India-native (built for Indian working rhythms, not translated from Western sleep content), which is precisely Off Hours' positioning for late-working Indian cities. Finally, the practice's corporate-wellness and clinic programmes are the natural on-ramp to the brand's Phase 2 partnership plan, with a partner who already holds the buyer's trust.

**Role in the strategy.**
1. A guided ten-night wind-down series for the private channel — one breath practice per night, no product in frame.
2. The pre-roll practice for the "Night 1 of 10" film (credited, disclosed).
3. A co-hosted evening-practice session at the end of the first ten-night cohort.
4. Introduction path into Phase 2 corporate wellness programmes.
5. Carries **The Landing**.

**Guard.** No medical or therapeutic claims about the product. The practice content stands on its own; the product is present as the ritual's object, never as the practice's subject.

## Before activation — the three checks

| Check | Status | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Category conflict** — confirm neither partner is currently contracted to a competing sleep or supplement brand | **Open — must be verified.** This materially affects both recommendations and is the single most likely reason to change them. | Partnerships |

| **Audience geography** — confirm the Mumbai + Bengaluru share of each audience, since Phase 1 is a two-city launch | Open | Growth |

## What we are not doing

- **No celebrity or macro-creator blitz.** Borrowed attention is the wrong trust mechanic for an ingested product, and it is a named non-priority in the Assignment 1 plan.
- **No trend-jacking specialists.** A creator whose audience expects stunts cannot deliver a bedtime ritual.
- **No follower-count-first selection.** A larger audience we cannot convert through trust is more expensive than a smaller audience we can.
