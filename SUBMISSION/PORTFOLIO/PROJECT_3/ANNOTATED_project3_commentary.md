## The adjustments, in plain English

The eight adjustments listed in SUBMISSION §3 are not arbitrary aesthetic preferences. Each comes from a specific evidence signal:

1. **Slime stays black.** The Dark Warrior deity on the Life Door is the dominant image at the outcome sector, and 九地 dark-concealment energy supports the early build. Black-on-dark is also dramatically easier to generate consistently in AI video.
2. **Leave AI jank in.** The presentation/display sector (Qian 6) carries "polish dies in the tomb" patterns — over-smoothing AI video wastes hours and produces something that looks dishonest next to this piece's own argument. Stop-motion is a form that forgives and even celebrates slight inconsistencies.
3. **30–45 seconds.** The haste-warns pattern (悖格 at Xun 4) punishes bloat. This is a single-idea film.
4. **One real artifact per role.** This is the "real problem / real learning" signal from 天芮 on the Life Door — weight comes from specifics, not archetypes. It also prevents the film from becoming a generic costume parade.
5. **End on designer.** The fire/visibility corridor (Li/South) makes the final form a display/design form; ending elsewhere wastes the strongest current. The "blink instead of break" beat is the piece's argument in one micro-gesture.
6. **No music, sounds only.** Sharp acoustic events (zap/click/splat/crackle) belong to the Shock/Metal door (惊门) at the ZhiFu command seat; composed music pushes the film into the polish-trap presentation sector.
7. **Solo production; one trusted pair of eyes, not collaborators.** The cooperation-blocked (Dui 7) warning is explicit: equal collaboration wastes resources and dilutes authorship. The Tai Yin (subtle hidden help) signal supports exactly one quiet feedback pass.
8. **Don't re-pitch.** The concept shown to the mentor ("AI-made, high quality, break/tweak/rebuild") is literally what this film is. No new approval is needed; asking for one just triggers the rework-waste current at Kun 2.

## Why this is still least-effort (and why it looks fancier than it is)

A 30–45 second AI-generated stop-motion short looks ambitious. In practice it is faster to produce than the text chapbook I previously proposed:

- You do not have to write 1000+ good words. You have to write 11 short prompts.
- You do not have to typeset, layout, or print anything.
- You do not have to shoot, light, or animate anything by hand.
- You do not have to compose anything (no music).
- The total edit is 11 clips, hard cuts, sound effects, one text card. Any free editor handles this.
- The "leave the jank in" rule is a time-saver, not a compromise — it means you do not fight the model for perfect output.

The honest production window is 2.5–3.5 hours. That is competitive with the text fallback, and the final artifact is substantially stronger on every grading criterion.
