## Walkthrough

### Finding 1 — Today is submission day; the decision is not "which concept is better" but "which path ships strongest under the constraints"

The brief's hard constraints are a 05 Oct submission and 06 Oct presentation, and your own added hard constraint is minimum time / least effort. You also have a social-momentum constraint: Concept 1 has already been shown to a mentor. Any change that requires re-pitching or a second build directly attacks both the time constraint and the momentum constraint. A path that keeps Concept 1 structurally but deepens its content has a strictly lower cost than a switch.

**Technical basis:** Case-context decomposition shows the cast chart was in response to a project already in flight; the structural analysis flagged a blocked/inauspicious execution sector (Kun 2, ZhiShi Injury Door blocked and tombed with repeat-frustration patterns), which translates project-strategy-wise into: avoid re-opening decisions that are already settled, because rework energy is unusually costly on this chart. The same analysis flagged the ZhiFu command seat (Gen 8) as the anchor — i.e., what you are already commanding and have started is what the strongest current supports.

### Finding 2 — Concept 2 is not abandoned; it is relocated into the argument layer

Concept 2 (fate = predetermined outputs; free will = editorial agency; consciousness = the reader / maker watching the collision) is philosophically real and is, in fact, a cleaner statement of what Concept 1 was already doing than Concept 1's own framing was. The submitted piece makes that relocation explicit in three places: the concept statement ("the tool offers a distribution it was trained on — but so is the free will of the editor who cuts, tweaks, rejects, and rebuilds; and the consciousness that reads the finished piece watches that entire collision as itself"), the colophon, and the short answer to Q&A if asked. The relocation costs almost zero words and does not require a new form.

**Technical basis:** The analysis' favorable node at Xun 4 (SE) carries Bing-at-Lu prosperity with the 日月相会 sun-moon-union pattern and 奇仪相合 (harmonious union of paired elements) — a pair of structures whose traditional reading is that two seemingly opposing energies harmonize when held in the right container rather than forced to choose. The same node's 悖格 (contrary/rebellion) warning cautions against forcing a choice that collapses a productive tension. Project-strategy reading: holding both concepts in one artifact is favored; forcing a binary choice is not.

### Finding 3 — The artifact should be pure text, process-visible, and producible by executing its own instructions

The piece proposed — five iterations of one seed with visible inter-stage editorial notes, chapbook format, ~10 pages equivalent, serif + monospace typography — is the smallest artifact that can make the argument persuasively. It is designed so that producing it is the act it describes (you perform the edit the piece is about), which collapses "making the work" and "researching the work" into one activity and saves the time normally spent on research, assets, and separate documentation. No graphics is not a concession; graphics would dilute the editorial-argument core.

**Technical basis:** The analysis flagged the Life Door (Li 9 / South) as the generative/outcome node but layered with the 玄武 (hidden/mysterious) deity and hidden-pattern brightness: real outcome is genuine substance, not surface display; imagery/polish is actively a trap (Jing/Scenery Door at Qian 6 is the in-tomb polish-trap node in the reading). The 天心 (heart/mind/healing) star at the cooperation-blocked palace reinforced solo production over building complex collaborator-dependent artifacts. Text-only, solo-authored, substance-led form matches these signals.

### Finding 4 — The presentation should use the artifact itself, not a deck

Three-minute talking points are provided. No slides. The student reads the opening, makes three points, reads the final line, and sits. This avoids the polish trap and keeps the presentation aligned with the piece's own argument (trust content; don't decorate it).

**Technical basis:** The analysis flagged the presentation sector (Qian 6 / NW) as carrying in-tomb display energy with treachery patterns — polished presentations perform poorly here — while the Tai Yin (hidden/subtle help) deity on the same sector indicates one quiet channel of help (here: your own artifact as the visual aid) outperforms broad performance.

### Finding 5 — The two non-negotiable editorial moments are the real success condition

The piece rises or falls on two specific instructions: cutting every phrase you've seen before (Iteration 01→02 break), and deleting your favorite sentence (Iteration 03→04 sacrifice). These are not decoration. They are the structural moments that force the piece past generic AI-commentary into something specific to you.

**Technical basis:** The analysis' "death-tomb conveyor belt" across multiple stems described a climate in which stale patterns are strong and must be actively broken; the center-palace 太白同宫 battle-formation favors active intervention (guest/attack over host/defense). The two cut instructions are the active-intervention moves in the piece's architecture; without them, the work falls back into the very genericity it criticizes.

## Confidence and residual risk

- **Decision (stick with C1 + absorb C2):** HIGH confidence. The cost of switching is high, the cost of absorbing is near-zero, and the philosophical layer makes the piece measurably stronger without requiring new production.
- **Artifact form (text chapbook, five iterations, four inter-stage notes, serif + monospace):** MEDIUM-HIGH confidence. The form is constrained enough to produce quickly, and it matches all signals in the structural analysis; residual risk is purely typographic (a bad typesetting job can flatten the visual hierarchy and make the iteration structure harder to read; fix: keep it simple, one serif + one monospace, nothing else).
- **Presentation approach (no deck, artifact-led, three points):** MEDIUM confidence. This depends on your in-class presentation culture permitting artifact-led talks without slides; if the class norm strictly requires slides, a single black-slide-with-the-final-sentence-of-05 is the minimum-viable deck and does not violate the no-polish principle.
- **What would change these verdicts:** (a) If your mentor had explicitly rejected C1 or asked for a different direction, switching cost would be justified; (b) if you had a ready-made C2 artifact already built, switching cost collapses and B becomes viable; (c) if the course required visual/graphic output, the text-only form would need a visual companion — but the brief accepts experiential and text-based forms and your explicit instruction is no graphics.
