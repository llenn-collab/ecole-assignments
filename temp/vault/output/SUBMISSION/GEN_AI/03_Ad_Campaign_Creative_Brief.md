## Campaign Concept (The Big Idea)
**"Proof of Morning."** Every frame of the campaign is stamped like the Batch Panel: real times, real dates, real temperatures — the ads read like production paperwork for the day itself. The coffee didn't just start your day; it *documents* it.

## Hero Shot Direction
Single image that carries the campaign: **a Vesper Black bottle, condensation beaded, held mid-air at exactly the height of a sunlit kitchen counter edge, in the blue-grey half-light of 6:47 AM.** Composition: rule-of-thirds left-weighted; bottle razor-sharp as the focal point with the Batch Panel legible (dates readable at print resolution); background falls away into a soft-focus domestic morning — a towel, a laptop closed but not packed, a window with the first hard light raking across the frame. In frame: one hand (manicure-neutral, a thin silver band), the bottle, the counter, the light. Mood: the two seconds *before* the day starts — calm, chosen, accountable. Overlay treatment: a single oxide-red caption line set like a lab slip — "BREWED MAR 16 · 18H · 4°C — DAY STARTS 06:47". Someone else must be able to shoot or generate this from this paragraph alone.

## Stage / Environment
Primary environment: **real locations, not studio** — scouted urban apartments/kitchens with strong east-facing morning windows (plus one late-afternoon interior for the 2:30 PM pivot support shots). Lighting: available-light base + one bounced fill; hard low sun as the protagonist; no diffusion haze, no food-styling gloss. Backdrop: honest surfaces — oak, tile, stainless, fridge door slightly ajar. Abstract/surreal environments are explicitly rejected for this campaign (they belong to the pastel wellness competitors). Secondary set-build: a **practical "batch wall"** for detail shots — pegboard with printed batch slips, filter paper, green beans — shot documentary-style; this doubles as retail-content backdrop.

## Models / Casting Direction
Who's in it: adults **27–38**, cast to mirror the researched audience exactly (urban Millennial/Gen-Z refrigerated-case buyers, cafe-grade expectations, no-guilt orientation): mixed ethnicity reflecting the researched metro split, understated personal style (workwear-adjacent, unbranded, matte textures), energy described by one word on set: **deliberate**. No gym-sweat hustle portrayals, no wide-eyed hype. Styling: muted layers against ink-blue palette; nails/hands camera-ready (the hand is a lead character). Faces share frames with hands and objects — per the research's casting codes, human presence anchors product interaction, never overshadows it. Where shots are product-only (detail/close-ups), that absence is a stated choice: the paperwork speaks for itself.

## Plan of Action — Creative Direction Sequence (assets in priority order)
1. **Hero image** ("Proof of Morning", as above) — pulls mood-board pins PB-01 (blue-hour kitchen photography), PB-05 (condensation macro cue from the research's "Condensation Cue" code), PB-11 (red ledger-stamp typography).
2. **Supporting/lifestyle ×3** — (a) the 6:47 boundary: person at counter, first light, bottle opened, neck band broken [PB-01, PB-03]; (b) the 2:30 PM pivot: desk-edge, bottle beside closed laptop, same red-caption grammar ["BREWED… PIVOT 14:32"] [PB-04, PB-08]; (c) the shared fridge-door shelf: household variety, three variants visible, no dialogue needed [PB-03].
3. **Detail / product close-ups ×4** — Batch Panel macro legibility test; break-the-seal neck band sequence (3 frames); condensation on clear glass showing liquid color; label tactile-substrate raking light [PB-07, PB-09, PB-12].
4. **Motion requirement** — 15s hero cut + 6s bumpers: static-frame documentary style, ambient sound only (fridge hum, cap crack, pour, street waking), red-caption timestamps tick live on screen; no VO until final frame ("Everyone says small batch. We print the batch."). Sound design follows the research's under-used restrained-ambient register rather than the category's escalating kinetic scores [PB-01, PB-10].

## Production Requirements
**Proposed path: hybrid — traditional photography core, AI-assisted extensions.** (Stated as a pick, per the brief.)
- **Traditional production (primary, non-negotiable for hero + details):** 1 photographer (commercial food/beverage, natural-light portfolio matching PB-01/PB-05), 1 assistant, location scout (2 permitted urban interiors + 1 set build), stylist, 2 models (day rate), props (batch slips, green beans, filter paper, practical fridge), post: retouching with a declared authenticity policy — no liquid alteration, no fake condensation; capture real pour takes. Budget realism anchor: 2 shoot days fits a regional premium-launch spend; the perishable product requires same-day cold-chain handling on set (coolers, backup stock ×3).
- **AI-generated production (extensions only, using the course-standardized image/video workflow):** stage 1 — text-to-image exploration of hero compositions from this brief's own paragraphs before booking locations (internal use, never published); stage 2 — generative video: motionize approved still hero into 6s bumpers (parallax, condensation drift, timestamp tick) via image-to-video tooling; stage 3 — localization variants (caption-language swaps, aspect crops for vertical platforms) through edit-assist tooling. **Human intervention points (quality/brand consistency gates):** every published frame passes (a) Batch-Panel legibility check — dates and numbers must be pixel-correct, AI text-rendering errors are disqualifying; (b) authenticity policy review — no generated product geometry, ever; the bottle is photographed, not hallucinated; (c) legal review of any claim-bearing pixels (process facts shown in ads must match the actual batch record shown); (d) final brand-voice sign-off by the strategist named in Brief 1.
- **Pre-flight:** trademark screen on the name; insurance/location permits; model releases covering AI-motionization of their likeness (explicit clause, current consent norms); allergen statement on Draft Oat assets.
