## Product Definition
**What it is:** cold-brew coffee brewed 18 hours at 4°C, never thermally pasteurized, sold refrigerated with a 21-day shelf life, in three variants at launch:
- **Vesper Black** — single-origin cold brew, no dairy, 200 mg caffeine. Hero SKU; the "no shortcuts" statement product.
- **Vesper Draft Oat** — cold brew + oat-milk emulsion, lightly sweetened (dates, 4 g sugar). For the researched plant-milk draft-latte demand curve.
- **Vesper Decaf Ritual** — Swiss-water decaf cold brew for the evening-boundary occasion named by the brand itself.
**Format:** 275 ml slim glass bottle (the format the research identifies as the premium-growth vector: glass in natural grocers/specialty at 34.2% CAGR), aluminum cap, paper label. A 310 ml recyclable slim-can line is scheduled as Phase 2 only if cold-chain distribution economics allow — the research notes cans dominate revenue (43.6–62.4%) but also that DTC cold-chain shipping weight structurally constrains glass; the bottle is a deliberate channel choice (refrigerated bay + DTC multi-pack), not an oversight.
**Size options:** single 275 ml; 4-pack and 12-pack DTC subscriptions (research: high-margin shelf-stable-style subscription multi-packs are the proven DTC discovery engine — ours is genuinely perishable, which is the point).
**Price positioning:** $4.50–$6.00 single-unit retail — squarely in the researched premium band ($4.50–$6.00 vs mass private-label at a 30–50% discount and legacy craft at $5.00–$7.00). Premium justified by disclosed production record, not matte varnish.

## Packaging Direction
- **Materials & structure:** clear flint glass (transparent = literal legibility of the liquid), matte uncoated paper label with a slightly gritty tooth (tactile-substrate code from research, under-used versus saturated soft-touch), tamper-evident printed neck band that must be broken to open (a physical "opened for the first time" ritual beat), screw cap in bare brushed aluminum — metallic restraint used once, never as decoration. How it opens: twist-break; how it's held: slim cylindrical grip, fridge-door native.
- **Color & finish, tied to a specific pin/insight:** palette pulls against the researched shelf uniformity ("neutral oat tones, muted pastels, metallic flourishes" everywhere): **deep ink-blue ground + warm cream typography + one oxide-red accent** reserved exclusively for dates and batch numbers. Mood-board pins PB-02 (ink-blue ceramic studies), PB-07 (cream letterpress swatches), PB-11 (red ledger-stamp macro). Insight IN-05 applied: since beige-on-beige no longer signals anything, the standout must be chromatic *contrast*, not more texture.
- **The one shelf-standout element (recognizable at three feet):** the **Batch Panel** — a fixed white rectangle occupying the lower third of the front of pack, formatted like a lab slip, carrying only facts: roast date / brew window / max process temp / best-by / lab-test lot number. On a bay of adjectives, a dated receipt is unmistakable. Nothing else on the FoP competes with it.

## Functional-to-Emotional Bridge
The breakable printed neck band and the date-stamped Batch Panel are functional choices — tamper evidence and inventory clarity — that carry the entire emotional positioning of Brief 1: opening a Vesper feels like breaking the seal on your own inspection report, and reading "never heated above 72°C · drink by Apr 6" delivers the calm, accountable intimacy the "candid, precise, quietly confident" voice promises. The pack makes the philosophy physically checkable: ritual and control, in glass and paper, not in a slogan. This closes the loop the research demands between functional packaging choice and emotional positioning (matte/technical codes exist in the category but nobody binds them to *proof*).

## Shelf / Competitive Differentiation
Placed against the five researched competitor archetypes:
- **vs La Colombe (Draft Latte, mass-scaled craft):** they sell flavor-led cans with heritage-lite minimalism; we sell a perishable, dated bottle whose FoP carries zero marketing adjectives. Difference type: claim regime (promises vs receipts).
- **vs Stumptown / Blue Bottle (legacy Third-Wave ambient lines):** their packs trade on origin prestige and acquired-brand nostalgia; ours deliberately names its factory process over its farm myth. Difference type: narrative axis (provenance vs legibility).
- **vs Death Wish / Super Coffee (functional/performance brands):** they lead with potency numerals (caffeine mg, macros); our numerals are process dates and temperatures — facts about making, not about dosing. Difference type: what the numbers mean.
- **vs Chobani-launched oat lattes (wellness-funnel brands):** they lean pastel wellness cues; our ink-blue/red ledger palette reads financial-document serious. Difference type: chromatic register.
- **vs private label (Trader Joe's / 365 / Good & Gather):** they reproduce the craft look at −30–50%; they cannot reproduce a verifiable batch record without a supply chain they don't have. Difference type: imitability — ours is operational, not graphic.
In one describable sentence: **the only package on the bay that looks like it came with paperwork — and actually did.**
