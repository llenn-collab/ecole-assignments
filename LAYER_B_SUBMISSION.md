# THE PRODUCT

# **GRIT**
### *A whetstone for people who make things.*

**Category:** a sharpening stone — the flat, unglamorous block that decides how every blade in the kitchen performs.

**One line:** *I don't cut. I decide where the edge goes.*

---

# 1 · THE POSITION — read this first

The whetstone is **not** a metaphor for cleanup. It is a metaphor for **judgement**.

This distinction is the entire concept, and it needs stating early and hard, because a careless version of this idea sounds like production work — the person who tidies files, fixes kerning, proofreads. That is not what a whetstone does.

**A whetstone determines where the bevel lies.** It sets the angle. Two people can sharpen the same knife on the same stone and produce a chef's edge or a ruined one, and the difference is entirely judgement about angle, grade and when to stop. The stone is the instrument of that judgement. It doesn't execute someone else's decision — **it is the decision.**

So the claim is:

> **Anyone can generate options. Deciding which 30% to remove, and where the edge should sit, is the higher-order skill — and it is the one that actually ships work.**

Divergent ideation is cheap and abundant. Discernment is scarce. Choosing *this* concept over *that* one, knowing which line to cut, knowing when a thing is finished — that is editorial leadership, and it is the job of a creative director, not an assistant.

**Say this out loud in the room.** The one-line inoculation, verbatim:

> *"To be clear — sharpening isn't cleanup. The stone decides where the edge goes. That's an act of direction, not production."*

Everything downstream — the USP, the audience, the copy — is written to hold this line.

---

# 2 · USP *(brief requirement: Functional | Emotional)*

**Functional.** I supply the judgement that turns options into decisions. Most teams don't lack ideas; they lack someone who can look at six directions and say *that one, and here's what has to go.* I set the angle and hold it — across a concept, a deck, a campaign — so the work arrives with a point of view instead of a compromise.

**Emotional.** People stop being afraid of their own rough drafts. Working with me, "this isn't finished" stops being a confession and becomes a stage. I make the unfinished state safe — the only condition under which anyone risks a brave idea.

**In one sentence:** *I decide where the edge goes, and I make the deciding feel safe instead of exposing.*

**The deliberate trade-off** (say it before someone else does): this is a claim about discernment, not volume. I'd rather be the person who made eight things undeniable than the person who made forty things exist.

---

# 3 · AUDIENCE *(brief requirement: their challenge + your role)*

**Who.** The person one rung above me — creative lead, senior designer, founder, account lead — accountable for the quality of work they didn't personally make.

**The challenge in their life.** They aren't drowning in bad work; they're drowning in work that is *nearly good*. Seventy percent there. Fixing it themselves is faster today and corrosive over a year, because they become the bottleneck and nobody around them develops judgement. They're exhausted by the last thirty percent and can't say so — complaining about "almost good" sounds ungrateful.

**My role in their life.** I own the last thirty percent as a **decision**, not a task. What reaches them is already resolved and defensible, so their attention goes to direction rather than repair. Over time I'm also the person their juniors learn the standard from — the stone the whole kitchen uses.

**What that's worth.** They get their judgement back, and their team develops some.

# 4 · IDENTITY BRIEF *(everything but the drawing)*

### 4.1 The mark

A rectangle whose top edge is very slightly hollowed — a shallow dip through the middle. That dip is the wear pattern a stone develops after years of use: the physical record of every blade it has set. **The logo is a rectangle with the evidence of judgement worn into it.**

**Construction — this must be exact.** The hollow reads as *deliberate* or it reads as a mistake, and there is no middle ground:

- Draw the dip as **one continuous cubic Bézier**, two anchor points at the rectangle's top corners, symmetrical handles. **Not** an arc, not a sagged line, not a boolean subtraction of a circle.
- **Depth: exactly 1/9 of the rectangle's height.** Deep enough to be unmistakably intentional; shallow enough to still read as a rectangle at 16px.
- Handles horizontal at both anchors, so the curve leaves each corner **perfectly tangent to the top edge** — no visible kink where curve meets corner.
- **Proportion 7:2 (width:height)**, matching a real bench stone.
- Test at 16px favicon, at 40mm on the pack, and at 2m on a projected slide. If the hollow disappears at 16px, the mark fails; if it looks like a sag at 2m, the curve is wrong.

### 4.2 Colour — with exact proportions

Achromatic system with **one confined point of red.** Proportions are fixed, not approximate:

| Role | Colour | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Primary — ink, type, mark | **Steel grey** | **33%** |
| Ground — deepest value, backs the hero shot | **Near-black, cold-biased** | **27%** |
| Substrate — the board itself | **Bone / raw grey chipboard** | **20%** |
| Contained accent | **Red** | **13%** |
| — | *green* | **excluded entirely** |

**The red rule, and it is a rule.** Red appears **only where metal encloses it** — the grit number inside a struck rule, a single foil-blocked line, the register mark. It never appears as a field, a background, or a word. Contained, it is the one point of heat in a cold system and the thing that stops the identity reading as timid. Released — a red panel, red type at size — it wrecks the whole argument. **One red element per surface. Never two.**

That 13% is also the austerity insurance: it proves the restraint is a decision, not a shortage of ideas.

### 4.3 Typography

**Scale, ratio 9:7 (1.286).** Sits between a major third and a perfect fourth — slightly unusual, which is the point: an identity arguing precision shouldn't use the default scale.

| Role | Size |
|---|---|
| Caption / legal | **7 pt** |
| Body | **9 pt** |
| Sub-head | **12 pt** |
| Heading | **15 pt** |
| Display | **19 pt** |
| Hero | **25 pt** |

**Baseline grid: 15 pt.** All margins and gutters are multiples of 15.

**Exactly two weights. Not three.** Regular carries all base text; Medium carries the visible top layer — mark, hero line, grit number. **There is no display weight, no light, no bold.** If a layout needs a third weight, the hierarchy is wrong, not the palette. Two weights is a structural rule of this identity.

**Tracking, by tier:**
- Hero / display: **−20/1000 em** (tightest setting)
- Body: **−10/1000 em**
- Captions: **0**

**Face:** one plain grotesque, all caps for the wordmark, tight tracking. No italic, no gradient, no bevel, no outline. GRIT is a hard word; let it be hard.

### 4.4 Material — where the craft has to show

Minimalism only survives a jury if the making is flawless. The spend goes into substrate and finish, not decoration:

- **Colorplan Dark Grey, 350gsm**, or **raw grey chipboard 2mm** for the sleeve.
- **Blind emboss an abrasive grain** across the sleeve — a real texture, so the pack is faintly rough in the hand. **The pack should feel like the product.** This is the single highest-value production decision.
- **Deboss** the mark rather than print it — the hollow becomes physical.
- Grit number in **red foil**, small, inside a struck rule. The only ink on the outer sleeve.
- Interior tray in bone uncoated, letterpress text — visible only once opened.
- Edges raw and visible. No lamination anywhere, no gloss, no spot UV.

**Explicitly avoid:** gradients, flourishes, any decorative element not carrying information, illustrations of a person, spark or shine graphics, drop shadows. The identity's argument is that quality doesn't announce itself — every ornament contradicts the argument.

### 4.5 Name rationale

*Grit* is the literal unit whetstones are graded in (#400 coarse, #1000 medium, #6000 finishing) — the product's own technical vocabulary, not a label applied to it. It is also the ordinary word for perseverance. And it is the rough texture that does the work: the part that feels unpleasant and is the entire point. One syllable, hard consonants both ends.

**Tagline: Nothing here is sharp yet.** True of the stone (no edge of its own), of the person holding it, and of me. Saying three things at once with four words is the metaphor doing its work indirectly.

*Alternates:* **WHETSTONE №1** (more literal, more premium) · **LAST PASS** (more direct, less object-led).

# 5 · PACKAGING — FINAL COPY

*Slim rectangular box at the stone's proportion. Sleeve over tray — it opens by sliding, a small drawing-out motion.*

### FRONT
> **GRIT**
> #1000 · MEDIUM
> **Nothing here is sharp yet.**
> Sharpening stone · for people who make things

### BACK
> **WHAT IT IS**
> A stone. It has no edge of its own and never will. Its function is to be harder, flatter and more patient than whatever is drawn across it.
>
> **WHAT IT DOES**
> Sets the angle. Decides where the edge sits and how fine it goes. Removes what shouldn't be there and leaves the shape that was already inside.
>
> **HOW TO USE**
> 1. Soak it. Ten minutes. It works wet — dry contact damages both sides.
> 2. Bring the rough thing. Not the finished thing. This is the wrong tool for finished things.
> 3. Set the angle first. Everything after that is just holding it.
> 4. Coarse first, fine after. Skipping the coarse pass is why the edge won't hold.
> 5. Stop before you think you should. Over-sharpening is a real failure.
>
> **CARE**
> Will hollow with use. Not damage — the record. A stone with no hollow has decided nothing.
> Do not store it somewhere impressive. Store it where the work happens.

### SIDE PANEL
> **CONTENTS** One stone. No instructions for the blade — that part was always yours.
> **GRADE** #1000. Coarse enough to change something. Fine enough not to ruin it.
> **WEARS DOWN WITH USE.** As intended.

### INSIDE TRAY — visible only once the sleeve is off
> You came here with something that was nearly right.
> That's the correct way to arrive.

### BASE
> GRIT · Made to be used up · Not for display

# 6 · MOCKUPS

*The brief doesn't specify a number; five is my call, kept low because the pack is the argument. Craft on these is where an austere identity is won or lost.*

1. **Hero, front.** Straight on, single raking light so the emboss and deboss actually read. Near-black ground. No hands, no props. **If the texture isn't visible, relight it.**
2. **The open shot.** Sleeve half slid off, inside line just becoming readable. The only shot with drama in it.
3. **In situ.** Real work surface — dust, a mug ring, a scalpel, tape. Slightly overhead. **Must not look styled.**
4. **The stone, unwrapped and worn.** Hollow visible, wet. Pays off the mark — place it directly beside the logo on the identity slide.
5. **Range.** Three grades — #400 / #1000 / #6000 — proving the system extends.

**Photographic rule:** cool light, real shadows, raking angles that reveal texture. No gradients, no floating renders, nothing at a jaunty angle. If a shot looks like an advert, reshoot it.

# 7 · THE 3-MINUTE PRESENTATION *(brief constraint: 3 mins)*

Tension first, product second. **Don't open with your name.**

**0:00–0:30 · Their problem.** "Every creative lead I've worked with has the same private problem. They're not drowning in bad work — they're drowning in work that's *nearly good*. Seventy percent there. And fixing it themselves is faster today and fatal over a year, because they become the bottleneck and nobody around them develops judgement." *Hold it.*

**0:30–0:55 · The turn.** "So I asked what I actually am in that situation. The honest answer wasn't the knife." *Reveal the pack. Two full seconds of silence.*

**0:55–1:25 · The product.** "GRIT. A whetstone. No edge of its own. Never the thing on display. Judged by one thing only — the edge it leaves behind."

**1:25–1:50 · The inoculation. Do not skip this.** "And to be clear — sharpening isn't cleanup. The stone decides where the bevel goes. Two people, same knife, same stone: one gets a chef's edge, one ruins it. The difference is judgement about angle and when to stop. That's direction, not production."

**1:50–2:25 · Identity and packaging.** The mark and what the hollow is. The two weights, the one red. Hand the pack to someone in the front row — **let them feel the grain.** Read **one** line of copy aloud. Only one.

**2:25–2:50 · The claim.** "What I'm offering isn't hours. It's the last thirty percent as a decision — so the person above me spends their attention on direction instead of repair."

**2:50–3:00 · Close.** "Nothing here is sharp yet. That's what I'm for." *Stop. No thank-you paragraph.*

**Delivery.** Slower than feels natural. Keep the two-second silence — it's the best moment you have. No apologising, no caveats. If challenged on the concept, don't defend it — explain the *choice*: you knowingly picked the unglamorous object, and that's the argument.

**If asked "isn't this just editing?"** — *"Editing is the visible part. The decision about where the edge should sit is the invisible part, and that's the part that's hard. I'm claiming the second one."*

# 8 · PORTFOLIO CARRY-OVER *(brief: "Big Picture")*

- **About Me.** Headline: *Nothing here is sharp yet.* Then two sentences. Not five.
- **Grades as navigation.** **#400 Coarse** — experiments and process. **#1000 Medium** — developed projects. **#6000 Finishing** — flagship work. A real organising system, and it lets you show unfinished work without apology.
- **Case-study structure, every time:** *what arrived rough → the call I made → the edge it left.* Note the middle beat is **the call I made**, not "what I did" — it forces every case study to be about judgement.
- **Contact.** *Bring me the rough one.*
- **The hollow** as a running device — a dip in a rule, a section break, a page-edge mark.
- **Writing rule:** describe what you removed and why, not what you added.

---

# 9 · AGAINST THE STATED GRADING CRITERIA

**Uniqueness — "creative ideation and connection with self."** An anti-hero object chosen deliberately over the impressive ones, with a stated trade-off (discernment over volume) rather than a list of strengths.

**Use of Metaphor — "beyond the common & predictable."** Past tool-and-sharpness into bevel angle, grit grades, the water, the hollow, wearing down, and being judged only by the edge left behind — each mapping to something real about how you work. It generates a portfolio structure, not just an image.

**Design & Copy — "branding principles, persona & tonality."** One typeface, two weights, a 9:7 scale on a 15pt grid, four values plus one confined red, uncoated embossed stock so the pack feels like the product. Copy is instructional rather than promotional — which is itself the positioning.

**Big Picture.** Ships with the portfolio system attached: navigation, case-study structure, About page, contact line, repeating device.
